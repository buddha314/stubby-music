# Biome characters: what each landscape's beings have in common

**Art direction, not science** (user, 2026-11-18): once the beings were in, find what each
biome's beings share and lean into it. Read from the being atlas (103 beings,
`docs/research/beings/`): for each biome, the traits most of its beings share and the ones
over-represented against the whole atlas, counting every being that lists the biome. The
four kinds of being (givers, voices, devourers, bargainers) are from the atlas's run without
music (`runs/no-music_no-sound/`). The figures are still **essences, not gods**
(`research-music-mythologies` D6), and every being used must be marked open in the catalogue.

Each biome gets a **character** (one line to design by), what its beings share, and how to
lean into it in figures, sound and danger. Everything below the data line is a proposal.

## Desert: the unseen

**Shared:** sand (56%), rock and wind; the most **unseen** forms (38%) and beings **heard
only**; lures; themes of the **journey and drought**; night. Beings: the humming sand, voices
that lead travellers astray, the whirlwind rider, the spirits of solitude, the thirst serpent,
the sand worm, the famine wind.

**Lean in:** desert beings are **sound and absence first**: a hum, a call off to one side, a
shimmer that might be a shape. Show them late and partly: heat haze, a ripple of sand moving
against the wind, a silhouette in the dust. This is the game's own opening, silent until it
is heard, so the desert sets the rule for how beings are met: by ear before by eye.
**Danger is losing the way**: voices that lead astray, thirst, the wind that buries.

## Chaparral: hybrids on the rocks

**Shared:** **hybrid** bodies (40%: bird-women, lion-goat-serpents, goat-men), stone, rock,
scrub, caves and cliffs; **performers**; themes of **death, longing and punishment**.
Beings: the singers on the rocks, the reed that was a voice, the piper who became a river,
the song that moves stones, the midday panic, the fire-breathing hill beast.

**Lean in:** **composite bodies** and **the rock as a stage**. Chaparral is where music is a
contest and has a price: the song that lures ships, the piper flayed for challenging a god,
the panic at noon. Outcrops read as natural theatres and figures perch on them. **Danger is
the song itself**: the most musical biome is the one where listening can kill. A natural
home for the amphitheatre.

## Grassland: under the open sky

**Shared:** **open grass** (93%) and **sky**; **animals and birds** (horses, wolves,
lightning birds); beings **seen** plainly (67%, the most of any biome); **benevolent and
dangerous in equal measure**, no voices and few bargainers; guardians and guides. Beings: the
instrument made from a beloved creature, the winged steppe horse, the wolf mother, the
egg of the world tree, the many-headed devourer, the one-eyed shepherd giant.

**Lean in:** **big creatures in plain sight**, readable at a distance against the sky:
herds, wings, a giant on the horizon. Little is hidden here; what you see is what comes.
Givers are animals that guide and carry. **Danger is visible and approaches**: something on
the skyline grows closer. The least musical beings, which makes the grassland the place
where the player's music most visibly changes things.

## Wetland: the drowning water

**Shared:** water (94%) and mud; lakes, rivers, marsh; **devourers** (59%) and **lures**
(35%); **death** (71%, the most of any biome), with love, grief and longing. Beings: the
water horse, the drowning imp, the pond hag, the singing water maidens, the river lady, the
swan maiden, the singers on the lake.

**Lean in:** **beauty that drowns**. Wetland beings are lovely or pitiful at the edge and
deadly in the water: reflections, a voice from the reeds, a horse that waits by the shore.
The sad singers belong here too (swans, maidens): the wetland is where grief is sung. **Danger
is the lure to the water's edge.** The lakes refill as the story moves along (`docs/design/
design-guide.md`, *The world map*), so its beings return with the water: dry, a wetland's
beings are absent; refilled, they wake.

## Broadleaf forest: faces in the grove

**Shared:** **human-like** forms (60%), shapeshifters, giants; wood and groves; **voices,
calls and laughter**; bargainers, givers and punishers; **hunger, bargains, protection and
warning**. Beings: the forest master, the moss folk, the devouring forest hag, the fox
spirit, the goblins who buy a song, the wild man of the woods, the forest that hides.

**Lean in:** **faces in bark, laughter between trees, a host with rules.** Broadleaf beings
deal with you: help for the polite, punishment for the rude, a song bought for a lump
removed. Figures are human-ish but wrong in one detail (a backwards foot, a hollow back, a
face in moss). **Danger is breaking the rules of hospitality.**

## Conifer rainforest: the deep wood

**Shared:** wood and groves (94%), **mist** and peaks; **roars** and calls; **guardians,
punishers and devourers**; **bargains, journeys and hunger**; night. Beings: the wild hunt,
the trolls turned to stone, the hollow-backed forest woman, the unburied child ghost, the
honoured bear, the snow woman, the teacher under the waterfall.

**Lean in:** **mist, scale and the hunt.** The deep wood is darker and bigger than the
broadleaf: the roar in the fog, the horns of the hunt overhead, stones that were trolls. Its
givers are stern (the bear, the forest master) and its bargains are hard. **Danger is being
hunted, or lost in the fog** (the forest that hides).

## Tropical forest: the mimic canopy

**Shared:** canopy, trees and groves; **voices** (47%); **lures and tricksters** (40% and
33%, the most of any biome); **shapeshifting and people turned to animals**; trickery.
Beings: the bird all others hush for, the mismatched-foot mimic, the river dolphin suitor,
the canopy musicians, the path twister, the backward-footed forest guardian.

**Lean in:** **nothing is what it sounds like.** Tropical beings imitate: a friend's voice,
a lover's form, a birdcall that is not a bird. Sound design should play with mimicry, calls
answering from the wrong place, and a canopy full of voices whose singers are never quite
seen. **Danger is being fooled.**

## Mountain: giants and their gifts

**Shared:** stone (60%), **peaks and caves**; **giants** (32%); **givers and teachers** and
**punishers**; **bargains and wealth**. Beings: the echo, the gift of the horn, the lord of
mountain weather, the hooded mine spirit, the trolls, the great healing bird of the peak, the
wild man of the snows, the birds who taught the modes.

**Lean in:** **great powers that give at a price.** Mountain beings are big and old and
trade: a horn for a choice, ore for respect, healing for a feather. The echo lives here and
the music teachers do too (birds, spring spirits), so the mountain is where the player
learns. **Danger is a bad bargain**, and the weather.

## Across the biomes

- **How you meet them:** heard (desert, tropical), seen at a distance (grassland), at the
  edge (wetland), face to face (broadleaf, mountain), in fog (conifer), on stage
  (chaparral). Each biome can teach a different way of encountering beings.
- **What danger means:** getting lost, the song, what approaches, the lure, rudeness, the
  hunt, being fooled, a bad bargain. Eight kinds of danger to grade toward the perimeter.
- **Music:** chaparral is by far the most musical (over half its beings); grassland and
  conifer the least (about a fifth). Where beings are least musical, the player's music is most visibly new.
