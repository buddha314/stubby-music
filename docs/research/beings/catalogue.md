# Being atlas: catalogue

Researched 2026-11-18 in four passes, two biomes each, under the `mythology-sources` rules. One entry per being coded in `beings.json`, with sources and the sharing check; restricted beings are listed and never coded. The first seventeen (the music essences) are documented in `../music-mythologies.md`. Merged as duplicates: the snow woman (conifer and mountain passes), the mountain crone into the devouring forest hag, the dancing rusalki into the singing water maidens, and the desert spring melody-giver into the spring that tunes instruments. Most sources were read as search excerpts; check primary texts before anything ships.

## Being atlas: desert and chaparral

Researched 2026-11-18. Builds on `docs/research/music-mythologies.md` Part 1 (its restricted findings are reused below).
Existing rows counted, not repeated: desert `humming_sand`, `answering_place`, `wandering_flute_player`;
chaparral `reed_voice`, `singing_insects`, `echo` (mountain primary).

**Counts:** desert 15 (3 existing + 12 new; 6 monsters), chaparral 14 (3 existing + 11 new; 4 monsters,
plus the dangerous spirit `mountain_fire_spirit`). Cross-listed: `desert_making_serpent` (also chaparral),
`humble_bird_brings_songs` (also desert).

### Desert

**Cultures looked at:** Arabian and Saharan (Bedouin lore, classical lexicography, Thousand and One Nights);
Tuareg; Roman literature on Libya and Cyrenaica (Lucan, Pliny); Assyrian and Babylonian; Silk Road travellers
(Marco Polo in the Lop desert); Mongolian Gobi; Chilean Atacama mining folklore; Aymara and Quechua altiplano;
|Xam San (Karoo); Hopi, Ancestral Puebloan, Southern Paiute, Cahuilla, O'odham, Navajo; Anangu (Central Australia).

- **The shapeshifter of the empty waste that lures and eats travellers** (`shapeshifting_desert_devourer`, monster) — Arabian ghul; Persian/Levantine ghoul tales.
  Takes the form of a hyena or an alluring woman (sometimes with hooves) and leads lone travellers off to be eaten; some medieval scholars called it fear and hallucination.
  Sources: [Wikipedia, Ghoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghoul) (encyclopaedia); [Britannica, ghoul](https://www.britannica.com/topic/ghoul) (encyclopaedia); [Medievalists.net, demons, djinns, devils](https://www.medievalists.net/2020/10/demons-djinns-devils/) (popular, academic author).
  Sharing: **open** — folklore recorded in published literature; keep it a desert monster, not a theological claim about jinn.
- **Voices and drums that call a straggler by name** (`voices_that_lead_astray`, spirit) — Marco Polo, Lop desert; travellers' lore noted by Yule.
  Stragglers at night hear spirits speaking like their companions, calling them by name, and are lost; by day, instruments and drums.
  Sources: [Digital Silk Road (NII), Toyo Bunko Marco Polo edition](https://dsr.nii.ac.jp/toyobunko/VIII-5-B2-9/V-2/page/0026.html.en) (museum/archive); [Classics Retold, Marco Polo ch. 34 (Yule translation)](https://classicsretold.blogspot.com/2025/02/marco-polo-in-central-asia-34-city.html) (popular, quoting primary); [Discover, singing dunes](https://www.discovermagazine.com/planet-earth/how-singing-sand-dunes-produce-desert-music) (popular science).
  Sharing: **open** — a 13th-century published travel text. Distinct from `humming_sand` (hum as the land's voice) because this is the luring, name-calling essence.
- **Half a body, one arm, one leg, hopping** (`half_bodied_hopper`, monster) — Arabian nasnas (Yemen, Hadramawt).
  Offspring of a shiqq and a human, or the transformed people of Iram.
  Sources: [Wikipedia, Nasnas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasnas) (encyclopaedia, citing Lane); [Ithraeyat, Denizens of the desert](https://ithraeyat.ithra.com/editions/desert/denizens-of-the-desert-nasnas) (museum: Ithra, Saudi cultural centre); [Pantheon.org, Nashas](https://pantheon.org/articles/n/nashas.html) (popular encyclopaedia).
  Sharing: **open** — published folklore and literature. Disposition coded ambivalent: sources do not make it a predator.
- **The red worm under the sand** (`sand_worm_that_kills`, monster) — Mongolian olgoi-khorkhoi (Gobi).
  Intestine-like red worm that surfaces in the hot months and kills by poison or shock; the Tartar sand boa is the likely animal.
  Sources: [Wikipedia, Mongolian death worm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_death_worm) (encyclopaedia); [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/46450-mongolian-death-worm.html) (popular); [Skeptoid 344](https://skeptoid.com/episodes/4344) (popular).
  Sharing: **open** — herder folklore widely published since the 1920s, not a sacred figure.
- **The snake whose bite brings endless thirst** (`thirst_serpent`, monster) — Roman dipsas (Lucan, *Pharsalia* 9); Greek seps.
  Born of Medusa's blood in the Libyan desert; its victim drinks a river and still dies of thirst.
  Sources: [Bestiary.ca, Lucan text](https://bestiary.ca/prisources/pstexts964.htm) (primary in translation); [Bexley, Medusa and Cato in Lucan 9 (ANU)](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/items/9bef28bb-0d6c-4f7d-b133-8f51eeed8954) (academic).
  Sharing: **open** — classical literature.
- **The serpent whose breath makes desert** (`desert_making_serpent`, monster; also chaparral) — Pliny's basilisk of Cyrenaica.
  Kills shrubs by touch and breath, scorches grass, bursts rocks; only the weasel defeats it.
  Sources: [Theoi, Basiliskoi (Pliny 8.33)](https://www.theoi.com/Thaumasios/Basiliskoi.html) (encyclopaedia quoting primary); [Wikipedia, Basilisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilisk) (encyclopaedia).
  Sharing: **open** — classical literature.
- **The famine wind** (`famine_wind`, monster) — Assyrian/Babylonian Pazuzu.
  King of wind demons, the southwest wind of drought, famine and locusts; his image also guarded against Lamashtu.
  Sources: [University of Saskatchewan Museum of Antiquities, Pazuzu](https://artsandscience.usask.ca/antiquities/collections/items/near-eastern-items/assyrian-items/demon-pazuzu.php) (museum); [World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/Pazuzu) (encyclopaedia); [Brewminate, J. Spier on Mesopotamian demons](https://brewminate.com/meet-the-demons-of-ancient-mesopotamia/) (popular, curator author).
  Sharing: **open** — dead religion known from excavated texts and objects.
- **The spirit riding the dust whirlwind** (`whirlwind_rider`, spirit) — Arabic zawba'a.
  The dust column is said to carry an insolent, rebellious devil.
  Sources: [Lane's Lexicon, ز ب ع](https://lexicon.quranic-research.net/data/11_z/012_zbE.html) (academic lexicon); [Middle East Eye, jinn](https://www.middleeasteye.net/discover/jinn-islamic-arabian-tradition-supernatural-beings) (popular).
  Sharing: **open** — a recorded folk belief about a weather phenomenon. Code only the whirlwind; leave out the named jinn chief Zawba'ah of Islamic tradition and later talismanic lore.
- **The ore-eating light bird** (`ore_eating_light_bird`, creature) — Chilean alicanto (Atacama).
  Glows gold or silver from the ore it eats; leads lucky miners to veins and greedy ones over cliffs (Chañarcillo, 1832).
  Sources: [Wikipedia, Alicanto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alicanto) (encyclopaedia); [Icarito (Chilean school encyclopaedia)](https://www.icarito.cl/?p=8966) (encyclopaedia).
  Sharing: **open** — mestizo mining folklore, nationally published.
- **The rain animal of the waterhole** (`rain_animal_of_the_waterhole`, creature; also grassland) — |Xam !Khwa.
  Sources and reasoning as in music-mythologies.md 1.4: [Digital Bleek and Lloyd](https://digitalbleeklloyd.uct.ac.za/cgi-bin/view/Curations/curatorium.zip/curatorium/pages/rain-power.html) (museum/archive); [de Prada-Samper 2019](https://folkloristics.org/pdf/2019/rain-stories-interpreting-water-beings-in-the-folklore-of-the-southern-african-khoisan-and-their-descendants.pdf) (academic).
  Sharing: **unclear** — San Council's code asks for consultation on commercial use of San knowledge; ask before use.
- **The people of solitude and longing** (`spirits_of_solitude`, spirit) — Tuareg Kel Essuf.
  Sources as in 1.5: [Rasmussen, Cultural Survival](https://www.culturalsurvival.org/node/3880) (academic); [Rasmussen, Oral Tradition](https://journal.oraltradition.org/wp-content/uploads/files/articles/13ii/2_rasmussen.pdf) (academic); [Round Head catalogue](https://roundheadsahara.com/rock-art/kel-essuf/) (academic).
  Sharing: **unclear** — living healing practice documented by an outsider; no community statement found. Never use the possession rite or its songs.
- **The spring that gives melodies** (`spring_melody_giver`, spirit; also mountain) — Aymara/Quechua Sereno.
  Sources as in 1.3: [Botero Villegas, Gazeta de Antropología](https://www.ugr.es/~pwlac/G20_13LuisFernando_Botero_Villegas.html) (academic); [UAB portal](https://portalrecerca.uab.cat/ca/publications/the-dark-world-and-the-music-in-the-andean-myths/) (academic).
  Sharing: **unclear** — living offering rite reserved to specialists; the pattern is low-risk, the ritual is not.

### Chaparral

**Cultures looked at:** ancient Greek (Arcadia, Lycia, Thebes, Thrace, Phrygia); Bronze Age Levant (Ugarit);
Portuguese and Galician; Moroccan coast (Gnawa); Kumeyaay, Cahuilla and Chumash (California); central Chile
(matorral, Mapuche-derived folklore); Khoekhoe (Cape fynbos); Noongar (SW Australia).

- **Bird-women singing on the rocks** (`singers_on_the_rocks`, monster) — Greek Seirenes.
  On a rocky isle among bones they sing a song promising knowledge, and sailors steer in to die.
  Sources: [Theoi, Seirenes](https://www.theoi.com/Pontios/Seirenes.html) (encyclopaedia quoting primary); [Wikipedia, Siren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siren_(mythology)) (encyclopaedia).
  Sharing: **open** — classical literature (see the Greek note in music-mythologies.md).
- **The fire-breathing beast of burning hills** (`fire_breathing_hill_beast`, monster) — Greek Khimaira; Yanartaş gas vents.
  Sources: [Theoi, Khimaira](https://www.theoi.com/Ther/Khimaira.html) (encyclopaedia quoting primary); [Wikipedia, Chimera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimera_(mythology)) (encyclopaedia); [Wikipedia, Yanartaş](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanarta%C5%9F) (encyclopaedia).
  Sharing: **open** — classical literature.
- **The night shapeshifter of lonely roads** (`roadside_shapeshifter`, monster) — Greek Empousa (and Lamia).
  Fiery hair, one bronze leg, shifting forms; seduces or devours travellers, fled from abuse.
  Sources: [Theoi, Empousa and Lamia](https://www.theoi.com/Phasma/Empousai.html) (encyclopaedia quoting primary); [Wikipedia, Empusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empusa) (encyclopaedia).
  Sharing: **open** — classical literature.
- **The midday panic of the wild pasture** (`midday_panic`, spirit; also mountain) — Greek Pan, *panikon deima*.
  Disturbed in his rest he shouts and herds stampede; herdsmen would not pipe at noon (Theocritus, Idyll 1). Kept distinct from `reed_voice` (Syrinx).
  Sources: [Theoi, Pan cult](https://www.theoi.com/Cult/PanCult.html) (encyclopaedia quoting primary); [EBSCO, Pan](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/social-sciences-and-humanities/pan-deity) (encyclopaedia); [Hutton, Gresham College lecture](https://www.gresham.ac.uk/sites/default/files/transcript/R_2025_09_04_0921_Hutton_T_FINAL.pdf) (academic).
  Sharing: **open** — classical; avoid showing worship rites of the small Hellenic revival.
- **Song that moves stones and trees** (`song_that_moves_stones`, spirit; also broadleaf) — Orpheus; Amphion.
  Coded as the essence of their song (they are legendary humans, not spirits).
  Sources: [Ovid Met. 10, Poetry in Translation](https://www.poetryintranslation.com/PITBR/Latin/Metamorph10.php) (primary in translation); [Ovid Met. 11, Theoi](https://www.theoi.com/Text/OvidMetamorphoses11.html) (primary); [Britannica, Amphion](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Amphion) (encyclopaedia).
  Sharing: **open** — public myth; do not use Orphic ritual texts.
- **The instrument that is a god** (`instrument_that_is_a_god`, spirit) — Ugaritic Kinnaru.
  Sources: [Franklin, *Kinyras*, ch. 7 (CHS)](https://www-current.chs.harvard.edu/chapter/7-kinnaru-of-ugarit) (academic); [Wikipedia, Kinyras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinyras) (encyclopaedia).
  Sharing: **open** — dead Bronze Age religion. No myth survives, so disposition is coded ambivalent as a placeholder.
- **The humble bird who brought the songs** (`humble_bird_brings_songs`, creature; also desert) — Kumeyaay Tu cuk.
  Sources: [Kumeyaay.com, Ron Christman](https://www.kumeyaay.com/traditional-origins-of-southern-ca-bird-songs.html) (community); [Smithsonian Folklife Festival](https://festival.si.edu/blog/2016/the-kumeyaay-communities-of-california/) (museum).
  Sharing: **open (story only)** — published by a community singer as a public text; never reproduce, sample or imitate the bird songs themselves.
- **The piper who became a river** (`piper_who_became_a_river`, spirit) — Marsyas.
  Sources: [Theoi, Marsyas](https://www.theoi.com/Georgikos/SatyrosMarsyas.html) (encyclopaedia quoting primary); [Wikipedia, Marsyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsyas) (encyclopaedia).
  Sharing: **open** — classical; do not depict the flaying.
- **The enchanted maiden of the spring and stone** (`enchanted_spring_maiden`, spirit) — Portuguese/Galician moura encantada.
  Bound at springs, wells, caves and old stones, combing her hair and singing, often a serpent; guards treasure; freed (or bound tighter) on St John's Eve.
  Sources: [Wikipedia, Enchanted moura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enchanted_moura) (encyclopaedia); [Algarve History Association](https://algarvehistoryassociation.com/en/features/portuguese-culture/207-small-myths-in-portuguese-culture) (popular, local history society); [The Portugal News](https://www.theportugalnews.com/news/2026-02-20/portuguese-magic-in-ancient-times/969321) (popular).
  Sharing: **open** — widely published Iberian folklore. The "Moorish" framing carries Reconquista othering; present her as an enchanted spirit of the place.
- **The flying head whose cry foretells death** (`death_cry_flying_head`, monster) — Chilean/Argentine chonchon, tue-tue.
  Sources: [Wikipedia, Chonchon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chonchon) (encyclopaedia); [Ancient Pages](https://ancientpages.com/2024/03/12/chonchon-tue-tue-bird-with-human-head-brought-bad-omens-to-places-it-haunted) (popular).
  Sharing: **unclear** — common Chilean folklore, but rooted in Mapuche beliefs about kalku sorcery, a living tradition; no Mapuche community statement found. Central Chile's matorral is the biome fit; it also ranges into the southern forests.
- **The banished spirit of fire on the mountain** (`mountain_fire_spirit`, spirit, dangerous; also mountain) — Cahuilla Tahquitz.
  Once guardian of shamans, turned to harm and banished to Tahquitz Canyon; seen as fireballs, lightning and meteors, heard as thunder.
  Sources: [Wikipedia, Tahquitz (spirit)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahquitz_(spirit)) (encyclopaedia); [Wikipedia, Tahquitz Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahquitz_Canyon) (encyclopaedia); [Visit Greater Palm Springs, The legend of Tahquitz](https://www.visitgreaterpalmsprings.com/blog/post/the-legend-of-tahquitz/) (popular). The Agua Caliente Band's own telling is at [tahquitzcanyon.com/the_legend](https://tahquitzcanyon.com/the_legend) (community; blocked automated fetch, not read).
  Sharing: **open** — the Agua Caliente Band of Cahuilla Indians tells the legend publicly at its own visitor centre, which exists so the tribe can tell the story before visitors enter. Read their version before depicting; keep shamanic practice out.

## Being atlas: grassland and wetland

Researched 2026-11-18. New rows are in `grassland-wetland.json`. Existing atlas rows are counted here but
not repeated. Source kinds: community, academic, museum, encyclopaedia, popular, primary.

### Grassland (grassland and savanna, steppe)

**Cultures looked at:** Eurasian steppe (Kazakh, Kyrgyz, Mongol, Oghuz Turkic, Göktürk), East Slavic and
West Slavic fields and steppe, the pampas (Argentine, Uruguayan, Guaraní), the northern Plains (Lakota,
Dakota), the southern African highveld (Zulu, Xhosa), the Queensland plains (settler and Aboriginal), the
Mande savanna and the Sahel (Hausa, Wodaabe, Maasai), with Arnhem Land savanna in the prior research.

**Count: 14** (3 existing: beloved_creature_instrument, music_against_death, courting_flute; 11 new).
**Monsters: 5** (many_headed_devourer, one_eyed_shepherd_giant, pressing_birth_demon, plains_werewolf,
lightning_bird); plus the dangerous noon_field_spirit.

- **noon_field_spirit**: Slavic Poludnitsa (Lady Midday), Polevik. A woman in white who strikes field workers at the noon of summer; shade keeps her off. Sources: [Wikipedia: Lady Midday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Midday) (encyclopaedia); [Wikipedia: Polevik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polevik) (encyclopaedia). Sharing: open, historical folk belief published since the 19th century (Erben, Dvořák).
- **many_headed_devourer**: Kazakh Zhalmauyz Kempir; Mongol epic mangus. The seven-headed or one-eyed devouring crone and the many-headed epic enemy, cut down and burned so it cannot regrow. Sources: [Wikipedia: Zhalmauyz Kempir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhalmauyz_Kempir) (encyclopaedia); [Nazarbayev Univ.: Monstrous femininity in Kazakh folklore](https://nur.nu.edu.kz/items/b694afd0-64cf-4034-aa14-6eda0e517b11) (academic); [Rinchindorji, *Oral Tradition* 16/2](https://journal.oraltradition.org/wp-content/uploads/files/articles/16ii/Rinchindorji.pdf) (academic); [Cologne Mongolian tales: characters](https://mongoltales.cceh.uni-koeln.de/index_characters.html) (academic). Sharing: open, published fairy tale and epic repertoire.
- **one_eyed_shepherd_giant**: Oghuz Tepegöz (Book of Dede Korkut). A one-eyed man-eater born of a shepherd and a fairy at a spring, bargained down to a daily toll by Dede Korkut, blinded and killed by Basat. Sources: [Wikipedia: Tepegöz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tepeg%C3%B6z) (encyclopaedia); [Columbia World Epics: Book of Dede Korkut](https://edblogs.columbia.edu/worldepics/project/the-book-of-dede-korkut) (academic). Sharing: open, a medieval epic, UNESCO-listed by its communities (see music_against_death).
- **pressing_birth_demon**: Kazakh/Kyrgyz albasty; Al. A female spirit feared at childbirth and the forty days after, which also presses sleepers. Sources: [Dallos, *Acta Ethnographica Hungarica* 2019](https://real.mtak.hu/128220/1/article-p413.pdf) (academic); [Wikipedia: Al Basty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Basty) (encyclopaedia). Sharing: open, folklore documented by ethnographers; protective practice (kuuču healers) not coded.
- **wolf_mother**: Göktürk Asena / Ashina. A she-wolf nurses the last boy of a massacred people and bears ten sons, founders of the Ashina clan. Sources: [Wikipedia: Asena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asena) (encyclopaedia, citing the Book of Zhou and Book of Sui); [Wikipedia: Ashina tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashina_tribe) (encyclopaedia). Sharing: open, a historical origin legend in Chinese chronicles. Note: a modern nationalist emblem (Bozkurt); portray only the wolf mother, no emblems.
- **winged_steppe_horse**: Turkic tulpar; Manas's Ak-Kula; Taiburyl. The winged or sky horse of steppe epic. Sources: [Wikipedia: Tulpar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulpar) (encyclopaedia, thinly cited). Sharing: open, epic motif and state emblem. Distinct from beloved_creature_instrument's winged horse, which dies and becomes a fiddle.
- **wandering_ghost_light** (also wetland, primary there): luz mala of the pampas; Min Min light of the Queensland plains. Sources: [Billiken: luz mala](https://billiken.lat/interesante/como-y-por-que-aparece-la-luz-mala-uno-de-los-mitos-argentinos-mas-famosos/) (popular, Argentine); [Wikipedia: Min Min light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_Min_light) (encyclopaedia). Sharing: open as settler and gaucho folklore; the Aboriginal stories behind Min Min were not looked into and are not coded.
- **plains_werewolf**: Argentine/Uruguayan lobizón; Guaraní luisón; lobisomem. The seventh son who turns beast on full-moon nights. Sources: [Wikipedia: Luison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luison) (encyclopaedia); [Buenos Aires Herald: Argentina's mythological creatures](https://buenosairesherald.com/culture-ideas/heralds-halloween-special-argentinas-mythological-creatures) (popular). Sharing: open, rural folklore. Origins disputed (Guaraní, Iberian, gaucho).
- **spider_trickster**: Lakota and Dakota Iktomi. The shape-changing spider trickster; sings for ducks to dance with closed eyes so he can catch them. Sources: [Zitkála-Šá, *Old Indian Legends* (1901), Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/338) (primary, by a Yankton Dakota author); [Wikipedia: Old Indian Legends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Indian_Legends) (encyclopaedia). Sharing: open, with care: published by a Dakota author for a general audience to preserve the tales. Use only those published trickster tales; Iktomi's role in ceremony is not coded.
- **lightning_bird**: Zulu and Xhosa impundulu. A great bird whose wings call lightning; a witch's familiar. Sources: [Wikipedia: Lightning bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightning_bird) (encyclopaedia, citing Berglund); [Dictionary of South African English: impundulu](https://dsae.co.za/entry/impundulu/e03242) (academic). Sharing: **unclear**: it lives in present-day witchcraft belief, and accusations around it still harm people; no community source on how it may be shared.
- **egg_of_the_world_tree**: Kazakh Samruk and Baiterek, with the dragon Aydakhar. The bird lays the yearly sun-egg in the tree of life; the dragon waits at the roots. Sources: [Kazinform: How giant tree legend turned into reality in Astana](https://www.inform.kz/en/how-giant-tree-legend-turned-into-reality-in-astana_a3286232) (community, Kazakh state press); [Advantour: Baiterek](https://www.advantour.com/kazakhstan/astana/bayterek.htm) (popular). Sharing: open, a public national legend (avoid copying the Astana tower).

### Wetland (marsh, bog, reed beds, lakes)

**Cultures looked at:** Scottish and Irish Gaelic, English (Lancashire, Old English), Swedish, East Slavic,
ancient Greek, Norse, Japanese, Nahua (Aztec), Mandinka and Gambian, south-eastern Aboriginal Australia and
colonial Australia, Argentine; plus from the prior research Finnish, Persian, Upper Rio Negro, West and
Central African water religion, Inner Niger Delta.

**Count: 15** (3 existing: singers_on_the_lake, reed_voice, beloved_creature_instrument; 12 new).
**Monsters: 9** (water_horse, drowning_river_imp, booming_billabong_beast, swamp_dragon,
weed_covered_pond_hag, regrowing_marsh_serpent, mere_dwelling_mother, crying_lake_water_dog, lake_otter_hound).

- **wandering_ghost_light**: will-o'-the-wisp, Irrlicht, Swedish lyktgubbe; luz mala; Min Min. Lights over bogs that lead walkers off the path, said to be fairies or souls. Sources: [Wikipedia: Will-o'-the-wisp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will-o%27-the-wisp) (encyclopaedia); [Folklore Scotland: Will o' the wisp](https://folklorescotland.com/will-o-the-wisp/) (popular); grassland sources above. Sharing: open, European and Latin American folklore.
- **water_horse**: Scottish kelpie and each-uisge; Irish aughisky; Swedish bäckahäst. A horse by the water that drowns its riders; steel or a bridle masters it. Sources: [Wikipedia: Kelpie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelpie) (encyclopaedia); [Wikipedia: Each-uisge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Each-uisge) (encyclopaedia); [DASG (Digital Archive of Scottish Gaelic) blog](https://dasg.ac.uk/blog/357/en) (academic). Sharing: open, published Highland and Nordic folklore.
- **singing_water_maidens**: East Slavic rusalki. They leave the water in Rusalia week to sing and dance on the banks; life-giving and dangerous. Sources: [Encyclopedia of Ukraine: Rusalka](https://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/pages%5CR%5CU%5CRusalkaIT.htm) (community); [Encyclopedia of Ukraine: Rusalka songs](https://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/pages%5CR%5CU%5CRusalkasongsIT.htm) (community); [Wikipedia: Slavic water spirits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_water_spirits) (encyclopaedia). Sharing: open (prior research). Music: core.
- **drowning_river_imp**: Japanese kappa. A water yokai of rivers, ponds and marshes that drags children and livestock under; bargained with cucumbers. Sources: [yokai.jp: Kappa](https://yokai.jp/en/yokai/kappa) (community, Japanese yokai portal); [World History Encyclopedia: Kappa](https://www.worldhistory.org/kappa/) (encyclopaedia). Sharing: open, folklore freely retold in Japan.
- **booming_billabong_beast**: bunyip (Wemba-Wemba / Wergaia word; many local beings). A swamp and billabong creature heard booming at night. Sources: [Wikipedia: Bunyip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunyip) (encyclopaedia); [Australian Geographic: The case of the roaring bunyip](https://australiangeographic.com.au/?p=152416) (popular). Sharing: **unclear**: "bunyip" is now general Australian folklore, but it stands over many distinct Aboriginal water beings whose stories belong to particular Countries; code only the generalised folklore figure, never a named local being.
- **swamp_dragon**: Gambian ninki nanka. A swamp dragon; seeing it brings death. Sources: [Wikipedia: Ninki Nanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninki_Nanka) (encyclopaedia); [Mongabay: Traditional belief in mythical beasts helps protect forests](https://news.mongabay.com/2012/06/traditional-belief-in-mythical-beasts-help-protect-forests) (popular). Sharing: open: folk belief told publicly, and Gambian communities now tell it on the Ninki Nanka Trails eco-tours.
- **weed_covered_pond_hag**: Jenny Greenteeth, Peg Powler, Peg o' Nell. A green hag under pond weed who drowns children. Sources: [Wikipedia: Folklore of Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore_of_Lancashire) (encyclopaedia); [The Fairyist: Jenny Greenteeth](https://fairyist.com/fairy-types/jenny-greenteeth) (popular, cites 19th-century glossaries); [Manchester Mill: Owd Jinny Greenteeth](https://manchestermill.co.uk/p/owd-jinny-greenteeths-comin-with) (popular). - **regrowing_marsh_serpent**: Greek Lernaean Hydra. Many-headed marsh serpent of Lerna, heads regrowing. Sources: [Theoi: Hydra Lernaia](https://theoi.com/Ther/DrakonHydra.html) (encyclopaedia of primary texts); [Perseus: The Lernean Hydra](https://static.perseus.tufts.edu/Herakles/hydra.html) (academic). Sharing: open, ancient religion known from literature.
- **mere_dwelling_mother**: Grendel's mother (Beowulf). A monster in a mere under the fens who avenges her son. Sources: [Open Book Publishers (chapter on the haunted mere)](https://books.openbookpublishers.com/10.11647/obp.0190/ch14.xhtml) (academic); [Wikipedia: Grendel's mother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grendel%27s_mother) (encyclopaedia). Sharing: open, Old English literature.
- **swan_maiden**: Norse swan-maidens, hagoromo tales, ATU 400. Bird-women bathe in a lake; a stolen robe binds one. Sources: [Wikipedia: Swan maiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_maiden) (encyclopaedia); [D. L. Ashliman: Swan Maidens](https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/swan.html) (academic, folktale texts); [vikingr.org: Lay of Völund](https://vikingr.org/old-norse-texts/lay-of-volund) (primary in translation). Sharing: open, worldwide published tale type. Distinct from singers_on_the_lake (cursed children who sing).
- **crying_lake_water_dog**: Nahua ahuizotl. A lake creature that cries like a baby to lure people in. Sources: [Wikipedia: Ahuizotl (mythology)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahuizotl_(mythology)) (encyclopaedia, citing the Florentine Codex); [Ancient Origins: Ahuizotl](https://www.ancient-origins.net/myths-legends-americas/ahuizotl-aztec-mythical-creature-or-real-fisherman-s-foe-009941) (popular). Sharing: open, from a 16th-century codex of a religion no longer practised in that form; not a worshipped deity.
- **lake_otter_hound**: Irish dobhar-chú, Glenade Lough. A giant otter-hound that kills by the shore, its mate following. ## Forest beings: broadleaf and conifer

Researched 2026-11-18 from web searches, mostly read as search excerpts; primary texts (Kalevala,
Mooney, Hearn, Bechstein) should be opened before anything ships. Rows are in `forests.json`.
Existing atlas rows counted here, not repeated: broadleaf `tree_harp`, `hidden_hill_music`;
conifer `waterfall_teacher`, `empty_mountain_music`, `tree_harp`.

Counts including existing rows: **broadleaf 14** (6 monsters), **conifer 17** (7 monsters).
Several rows span both forests; the first biome listed is the primary one.

### Broadleaf (temperate deciduous and mixed forest)

**Cultures looked at:** East Slavic, German, English, Greek, Basque, Korean, Chinese, Japanese,
Cherokee; also Haudenosaunee and Anishinaabe (restricted, below). Previous pass: Irish, Welsh.

- **forest_master: the master of the forest who keeps its game and leads travellers astray**
  (spirit; broadleaf, conifer). East Slavic leshy; Finnish Tapio and Mielikki. The forest's owner
  is heard laughing, whistling and calling, misleads those who offend him, and is sung to for game;
  the Kalevala herding charm asks the forest for a horn and honey-flute.
  Sources: [Britannica, leshy](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/337093) (encyclopaedia);
  [Tapio (spirit)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapio_(spirit)) (encyclopaedia);
  [Kalevala Rune 32, Crawford](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/elias-lonnrot/the-kalevala/john-martin-crawford/text/chapter-32) (primary).
  Sharing: **open**, published 19th–20th-century folklore and folk poetry; modern pagan honouring does not restrict it.
- **wild_hunt: the ghostly hunt that storms over the forest at night** (monster; broadleaf,
  conifer). German Wilde Jagd, Scandinavian Odin's hunt, English Herne and the Gabriel Hounds.
  Riders, hounds and horns pass over the woods on winter storm nights; meeting them brings death or war.
  Sources: [Wild Hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_Hunt) (encyclopaedia);
  [norse-mythology.org](https://norse-mythology.org/the-wild-hunt/) (popular);
  [Mythopedia, Herne](https://mythopedia.com/topics/herne-the-hunter/) (popular).
  Sharing: **open**, European folklore in Grimm and later collections. The Herne–Odin link is disputed.
- **forest_hag_who_devours: the old woman alone in the woods who feeds travellers or eats them**
  (monster; broadleaf, conifer). Slavic Baba Yaga; Japanese yamauba. Offers shelter, eats
  children or travellers, rewards the kind or those who pass her tests.
  Sources: [Britannica, Baba Yaga](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/47337) (encyclopaedia);
  [World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/Baba_Yaga/) (encyclopaedia);
  [Yamauba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamauba) (encyclopaedia);
  [BYU Bakemono, yamamba](https://bakemono.lib.byu.edu/yokai/yamamba/) (academic).
  Sharing: **open**, folktale literature.
- **wolf_skinned_man: the person who becomes a wolf at night in the forest** (monster; broadleaf,
  conifer). European werewolf, loup-garou, vukodlak/wilkołak.
  Sources: [Britannica, werewolf](https://www.britannica.com/art/werewolf) (encyclopaedia);
  [Werewolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werewolf) (encyclopaedia).
  Sharing: **open**, pan-European folklore and trial records.
- **soul_in_the_tree: the spirit whose life is one old tree** (spirit; broadleaf, conifer).
  Greek hamadryads (Erysichthon's tree bleeds and its nymph cries out; Demeter curses him with
  hunger); Japanese kodama (cutting its tree brings misfortune). Kept apart from the mountain
  `echo` row: this is the tree's life, not its answer.
  Sources: [Kodama (spirit)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodama_(spirit)) (encyclopaedia);
  [Bulfinch, ch. 22](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/thomas-bulfinch/bulfinchs-mythology/text/chapter-1-22) (primary/popular retelling);
  [Mythopedia, dryads](https://mythopedia.com/topics/dryads-and-hamadryads/) (popular).
  Sharing: **open**, classical literature and Edo folklore. Shrine trees with shimenawa are living
  worship: no shrine regalia, no Ghibli kodama look.
- **fox_spirit_shapeshifter: the old fox that takes human shape to feed on people** (monster;
  broadleaf). Korean gumiho; Chinese huli jing (Shan Hai Jing, Liaozhai).
  Sources: [Kumiho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumiho), [Huli jing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huli_jing),
  [Nine-tailed fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine-tailed_fox) (encyclopaedia).
  Sharing: **open** for the folktale fox. Japanese kitsune left out deliberately: as Inari's
  messengers they belong to living shrine worship.
- **moss_folk: small wood people of the moss who teach healing herbs** (spirit; broadleaf,
  conifer). German Moosleute, Holzfräulein, Moosweibchen; prey of the Wild Hunt.
  Sources: [Moss people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moss_people) (encyclopaedia);
  [Bechstein, *Deutsches Sagenbuch*](https://projekt-gutenberg.org/authors/ludwig-bechstein/books/deutsches-sagenbuch/chapter/548) (primary).
  Sharing: **open**, 19th-century German legend collections. The plague-herb detail is thinly sourced.
- **wild_man_of_the_woods: the hairy wild man who guards the flocks and warns of storms**
  (creature; broadleaf, mountain). Medieval woodwose; Basque Basajaun.
  Sources: [Wild man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_man), [Basajaun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basajaun) (encyclopaedia);
  [Buber's Basque Page](https://buber.net/Basque/2021/03/28/basque-fact-of-the-week-the-basajaunak-the-wild-lords-of-the-forest/) (popular/community).
  Sharing: **open**, medieval art and published Basque folklore (Barandiarán).
- **goblins_who_buy_a_song: night revellers in the woods who reward a good song or dance**
  (spirit; broadleaf, mountain; music core). Korean dokkaebi (*Hokbu-ri yeonggam*); Japanese oni of
  *Kobutori Jiisan* (Uji Shūi Monogatari, 13th c.). They take the old man's lump for his singing or
  dancing; the envious copier gets two.
  Sources: [Kobutori Jiisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobutori_Jiisan) (encyclopaedia);
  [St Olaf, variation in Kobutori](https://pages.stolaf.edu/crepepaperbooks/?p=2180) (academic);
  [Dokkaebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dokkaebi) (encyclopaedia).
  Sharing: **open**, children's folktales. Korean details come from secondary sites; the Korean
  version's colonial-era entanglement with the Japanese tale is worth noting in any design.
- **maidens_who_dance_to_death: drowned maidens who roam the woods and dance or tickle men to
  death** (monster; broadleaf, wetland). East Slavic rusalki, forest-and-field rusalki of Belarus,
  Rusalka Week.
  Sources: [Rusalka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusalka), [Rusalka Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusalka_Week) (encyclopaedia).
  Sharing: **open**, ethnographic folklore. Wetland pair may also code a rusalka; merge if so.
- **stone_skinned_liver_eater: the stone-skinned old woman of the mountain woods who steals
  livers** (monster; broadleaf, mountain). Cherokee U'tlun'ta (Spearfinger), via Mooney 1900.
  Sources: [Spearfinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spearfinger) (encyclopaedia);
  [World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/2585/utlunta-the-spear-finger/) (encyclopaedia).
  Sharing: **unclear**. Published openly for 125 years and retold regionally, but known mainly through
  an outsider's ethnography of a living nation; no Cherokee guidance found. Her song is not to be
  reproduced. Ask the Museum of the Cherokee People or EBCI before use, as for the Little People.

  [Scandinavian Archaeology](https://www.scandinavianarchaeology.com/children-of-the-night/) (popular).
  Sharing: **open**, folklore. Handle with care: it is about infanticide. The "grows heavier" motif is weakly sourced and not coded.
- **trolls_turned_to_stone: the huge forest and mountain giants who turn to stone in daylight**
  (monster; conifer, mountain). Norwegian and Swedish trolls, Asbjørnsen and Moe.
  Sources: [Troll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troll) (encyclopaedia);

  [Sons of Norway presentation](https://www.sofn.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/MiniPres301.pdf) (community).
  Sharing: **open**, national folktale collections.
- **maned_forest_serpent: the giant limbless serpent among the stones deep in the forest**

  Sources: [Lindworm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindworm) (encyclopaedia);
  [Unga Fakta, Svenska oknytt: Lindormar](https://www.ungafakta.se/kunskapsbanken/svenska-oknytt/material/Svenska%20oknytt,%20Lindormar.pdf) (popular/educational, Swedish).
  Sharing: **open**, folklore.
- **honoured_bear: the bear, kin to people, born in the sky and sung home after the hunt**
  (creature; conifer). Finnish Otso, Kalevala Rune 46, peijaiset feast songs.
  Sources: [Otso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otso) (encyclopaedia);

  [Bear worship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bear_worship) (encyclopaedia).

- **snow_woman: the pale woman of the blizzard who freezes travellers in the snowbound woods**

### Tropical (tropical moist forest)

**Cultures looked at:** Brazilian and Peruvian Amazon (Tupi-derived Brazilian folklore,
ribeirinho river folklore, Quechua-named Amazon lore, Mura); K'iche' and Yucatec Maya;
Akan (Asante); Mbuti and Aka; Malay, Minangkabau and Indonesian; Philippine (Tagalog and
wider); Temiar; Kaluli (existing row); Tukano, Baniwa and Baré (restricted only); Yoruba
(left out); Congo basin (Mokele-mbembe looked at, left out).

**The bird all others fall silent to hear** (`bird_all_others_hush_for`, creature) — Brazilian Amazon uirapuru.
When it sings, all other birds stop to listen; its song brings love and luck. The real musician wren sings in consonant intervals.
Sources: [Interlude on Villa-Lobos's *Uirapuru*](https://interlude.hk/the-legends-and-sounds-of-amazonia-heitor-villa-lobos-and-philip-glass/) (popular); [eBird, musician wren](https://ebird.org/species/muswre2) (scientific).
Sharing: **open** — Brazilian popular folklore and a composer's retelling; no Indigenous source on an original form was found. Leave out the belief about killing the bird for luck.

**The forest tree whose voice speaks through the drum** (`tree_that_speaks_as_drum`, spirit) — Akan (Asante) atumpan.
The talking drum is carved from a forest tree that received an offering before felling; the drummer first addresses the tree and each material, so the drum speaks with the tree's voice.
Sources: [Royal Museum for Central Africa, atumpan](https://music.africamuseum.be/instruments/english/ghana/ashanti/atumpan.html) (museum); [Ghana News Agency](https://gna.org.gh/2023/03/talking-drums-in-contemporary-ghana-the-atumpan-in-focus/) (community press); [Journal of African Music](https://journal.ru.ac.za/index.php/africanmusic/article/view/225/164) (academic).
Sharing: **open** for the general idea, taught publicly by the Akan scholar Nketia. The court invocation texts belong to a living chieftaincy institution and are not used.

**The forest that sleeps and is sung awake** (`forest_sung_awake`, spirit) — Mbuti (Ituri), as published; Aka polyphony.
The forest is a caring parent that "falls asleep" when misfortune comes, and nightly interlocking song wakes it.
Sources: [Encyclopedia.com, Efe and Mbuti](https://www.encyclopedia.com/international/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/efe-and-mbuti) (encyclopaedia); [HRAF summary of Turnbull](https://ehrafworldcultures.yale.edu/cultures/fo04/summary) (academic); [UNESCO, Aka polyphony](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/polyphonic-singing-of-the-aka-pygmies-of-central-africa-00082?RL=00082) (intergovernmental).
Sharing: **unclear** — the idea is published by an outside author (Turnbull) and it is not known whether Mbuti endorse that. The ceremony and its trumpet are restricted (below); depict no people, trumpets or masks.

**The backward-footed guardian who whistles hunters astray** (`backward_footed_forest_guardian`, spirit) — Brazilian curupira.
A small red-haired forest being with backward feet whose tracks mislead; with whistles and mimicked voices it loses hunters and woodcutters who take too much. First recorded by Anchieta, 1560.
Sources: [Wikipedia, Curupira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curupira) (encyclopaedia); [Language Lizard](https://blog.languagelizard.com/2014/09/10/international-folktale-character-curupira/) (popular).
Sharing: **open** — national Brazilian folklore, made São Paulo's forest-guardian symbol by state law (1970).

**The one-eyed forest giant with a mouth in its belly** (`belly_mouthed_forest_giant`, monster) — Amazonian mapinguari.
A huge shaggy beast, often one-eyed with backward feet and a mouth on its belly; older tellings make it a shaman turned beast. Feared, and also called a protector of the forest.
Sources: [Wikipedia, Mapinguari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapinguari) (encyclopaedia); [PBS *Monstrum*](https://www.pbs.org/video/mapinguari-fearsome-beast-and-protector-of-the-amazon-xnse9d) (popular, public broadcaster).
Sharing: **open** — widely published Amazonian folklore. Tellings vary; the shaman origin is one strand only.

**The river dolphin who comes ashore as a dancing stranger** (`river_dolphin_suitor`, creature) — Amazonian boto / encantado.
The pink river dolphin becomes a charming man in white, hat over his blowhole, who dances at riverside festivals and seduces women before returning to the river.
Sources: [National Geographic, river dolphins](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/magazine/article/dolphins-1) (popular); [Slater, *Dance of the Dolphin*, U. Chicago Press](https://chicagoreference.com/ucp/books/book/chicago/D/bo3632345.html) (academic; not opened).
Sharing: **open** — Brazilian river folklore, studied and published from community testimony. Note the real animal is endangered.

**The lady of the river whose voice draws men under** (`river_lady_who_drowns`, monster) — Brazilian Iara; older Tupi ipupiara.
A river woman whose voice lures men to drown; the seductive form is said to have grown in the 18th–19th centuries out of the ipupiara, a fish-man who devoured fishers.
Sources: [Wikipedia, Iara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iara_(mythology)) (encyclopaedia; cites Câmara Cascudo).
Sharing: **open** — national folklore. Origins disputed (Indigenous, European siren, Iemanjá influence); code only the shared core. Only one solid source; another would help.

**The forest double with one mismatched foot** (`mismatched_foot_mimic`, monster) — Peruvian and Brazilian Amazon chullachaqui.
Takes the face and voice of a loved one to lead people deeper until lost; its unmatched foot gives it away. Also punishes misuse of the forest.
Sources: [Wikipedia, Chullachaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chullachaki) (encyclopaedia); [Kuoda Travel](https://www.kuodatravel.com/blog/spooky-peru-the-myth-of-the-chullachaqui-in-the-amazon/) (popular, Peruvian).
Sharing: **open** — Peruvian regional folklore, freely retold.

**The hidden people heard but never seen** (`hidden_people_heard_singing`, spirit) — Malay, Minangkabau, Indonesian orang bunian.
An invisible people of deep forest and high hills, known by voices, singing, laughter or whistling with no one there; they may take lost children or marry humans.
Sources: [Yahoo Malaysia, citing a Universiti Malaya academic](https://malaysia.news.yahoo.com/blogs/yahoo-malaysia-newsroom/orang-bunian-neighbours-supernatural-world-072037643.html) (popular); [Ancient Pages](https://ancientpages.com/2016/07/15/orang-bunian-mysterious-invisible-whistle-people-living-forests-malay-folklore) (popular).
Sharing: **open** — common Malay folklore. The music detail rests on popular sources only; no ethnography found for it.

**The giant who sits smoking in the great fig tree** (`tree_giant_smoking`, spirit) — Philippine kapre.
A tall hairy giant in balete trees, betrayed by his cigar's glow; mostly watchful, sometimes a prankster.
Sources: [Wikipedia, Kapre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapre) (encyclopaedia); [Pantheon.org](https://pantheon.org/articles/k/kapre.html) (encyclopaedia, popular).
Sharing: **open** — national folklore (the Malacañang balete's kapre is a public joke). The cigar is post-contact.

**The horse-headed one who walks travellers in circles** (`horse_headed_path_twister`, monster) — Philippine tikbalang.
A long-limbed horse-headed being of mountain forests who leads lone travellers in circles; a shirt worn inside out breaks the spell.
Sources: [Wikipedia, Tikbalang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tikbalang) (encyclopaedia).
Sharing: **open** — national folklore. One source only; Philippine folklore collections (e.g. the Aswang Project) would strengthen it.

**The ghost woman announced by the scent of frangipani** (`frangipani_ghost_of_banana_grove`, monster) — Malay pontianak; Indonesian kuntilanak.
The ghost of a woman who died in childbirth (earliest: a stillborn child) who rests in banana trees and hunts at night, announced by frangipani scent.
Sources: [USC Folklore Archives, ghosts in banana trees](https://folklore.usc.edu/?p=42727) (academic archive, student-collected); [Yahoo Malaysia](https://malaysia.news.yahoo.com/legend-of-the-pontianak-a-look-into-her-history-legacy-233046685.html) (popular).
Sharing: **open** — widespread folklore and a film genre. Touches maternal death; keep it away from real grief.

**The woman waiting under the ceiba who leads men into the forest** (`woman_beneath_the_ceiba`, monster) — Yucatec Maya Xtabay.
A woman by a ceiba tree who lures men into the forest; in some tellings she becomes a serpent and devours them. The danger of the ceiba may be a colonial overlay on a sacred tree.
Sources: [Wikipedia, Xtabay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xtabay) (encyclopaedia); [Yucatán Today](https://yucatantoday.com/en/the-legend-of-xtabay/) (popular, regional).
Sharing: **open** — regional folklore retold publicly in Yucatán. The ceiba is sacred in Maya cosmology; do not cast the tree itself as evil.

**Restricted, not coded (tropical):** Mbuti *molimo* ceremony and trumpet (women and children may not see it); Jurupari / Kowai sacred flutes of the Tukano, Baniwa and Baré (forbidden to women and non-initiates); Aka masked forest spirits such as Ejengi (initiation-bound); Temiar dream-song spirit guides (songs belong to the medium and are used in healing ceremonies; unclear leaning restricted); Kaluli *gisalo* ceremony (left out of the existing muni-bird row). **Left out as living deities or practice:** Àyàn Agalú (Yoruba orisha of the drum), Balinese and Javanese gamelan spirits, K'iche' calendar day-signs. **Left out on sourcing or fit:** Mokele-mbembe (origin argued to be colonial rather than Congolese), Anansi (a story character, not a being of the forest).

### Mountain (rock, alpine meadow, mountain lakes)

**Cultures looked at:** Quechua and Aymara (Bolivian and Peruvian Andes); Ethiopian highlands;
Himalaya (Hindu and Buddhist kinnara; Sherpa and Bhutanese); Japanese mountain folklore;
Swiss and Alpine (Pilatus, Tatzelwurm, Ladin salvans); Giant Mountains (Czech, German,
Polish); German mining regions (Harz, Erzgebirge); Scottish Highlands; Persian epic
(Alborz); Philippine mountains (via tikbalang and orang bunian); Rocky Mountains (nothing
shareable found).

**The spirit of the spring that tunes instruments and gives new tunes** (`spring_that_tunes_instruments`, spirit) — Quechua and Aymara sirinu, Northern Potosí.
Water beings of springs, waterfalls and rocks; musicians leave new instruments overnight to be tuned and receive new melodies by rivers in the wet season. Also blamed for excess and abduction.
Sources: [Correo del Sur (2020)](https://correodelsur.com/ecos/20200301/chuturkurikasqani-trance-el-encantamiento-de-las-sirinus.html) (Bolivian press); [Stobart 2023](https://pure.royalholloway.ac.uk/files/56130162/Stobart_2023_Beyond_Pulsating_Dissonance_-_prepublication_version.pdf) (academic).
Sharing: **unclear** — a living practice; the sites are secret and recording is said to be dangerous. Use only the general idea; consult Andean advisers.

**Three mountain birds who taught the song's modes** (`birds_who_taught_the_modes`, creature) — Ethiopian highland tradition of Yared's chant (motif only).
Three birds from paradise taught a singer the three chant modes; some say birdsong falls into them.
Sources: [Mahibere Kidusan, Feast of Saint Yared](https://eotcmk.org/e/feast-of-saint-yared/) (community, church-affiliated); [Wikipedia, Yared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yared) (encyclopaedia).
Sharing: **unclear** — the church tells it openly, but it concerns a venerated saint and living liturgy. Only the unnamed bird motif; no saint, icons or zema.

**The bird-bodied singers of the high mountains** (`bird_human_mountain_singers`, creature) — Hindu and Buddhist kinnara and kinnari.
Half-bird, half-human musicians of the Himalaya, devoted pairs known for song and flute.
Sources: [Rubin Museum, Project Himalayan Art](https://projecthimalayanart.rubinmuseum.org/glossary/kinnara/) (museum); [Wikipedia, Kinnara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinnara) (encyclopaedia).
Sharing: **open** — a mythic class in published literature (Jatakas, Kalidasa), not a worshipped deity. Avoid Buddhist iconography and copying temple reliefs.

**The wild man of the snows, best avoided** (`wild_man_of_the_snows`, monster) — Sherpa yeti / metoh-kangmi; Bhutanese migoi.
A large hairy wild man of the high Himalaya, best avoided; in a Khumbu tale it takes yaks and returns them for gifts at its cave.
Sources: [Geographical](https://geographical.co.uk/culture/tracking-down-the-yeti-in-the-himalaya) (popular, magazine); [Nepal Traveller](https://www.nepaltraveller.com/sidetrack/legends-of-the-yeti-thursday-tales) (popular, Nepali).
Sharing: **open** — shared widely by Sherpa and Bhutanese communities themselves (tourism, stamps). Avoid monastery relics and Buddhist imagery.

**The pale woman of the snowstorm who leaves no footprints** (`snow_woman`, monster) — Japanese yuki-onna.
A deathly pale woman who freezes lost travellers; in Hearn's version she spares one on a promise of silence and leaves when he breaks it.
Sources: [Samurai Archives wiki](https://samurai-archives.com/wiki/Yuki-Onna) (popular); [Uncanny Japan, Hearn's text](https://uncannyjapan.com/podcast/yuki-onna-by-lafcadio-hearn/) (popular).
Sharing: **open** — Edo-period and earlier yōkai folklore. Hearn's specific tale may be his own; use the folk core.

**The old woman of the mountain hut who feeds or devours** (`mountain_crone`, monster) — Japanese yamauba.
A wild old woman in a mountain hut who shelters and then devours travellers, yet also guides the lost and fostered Kintaro.
Sources: [Wikipedia, Yama-uba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yama-uba) (encyclopaedia); [Asian Ethnology 78-2](https://asianethnology.org/articles/2223) (academic); [BYU Bakemono](https://bakemono.lib.byu.edu/yokai/yamamba/) (library).
Sharing: **open** — medieval-onward published folklore.

**The cat-faced worm of the stony slopes and tunnels** (`cat_faced_tunnel_worm`, monster) — Alpine Tatzelwurm; Swiss Stollenwurm.
A short serpent-bodied beast with a cat's face living in crevices and mine tunnels; shrieks or hisses, said to be venomous.
Sources: [Wikipedia, Tatzelwurm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatzelwurm) (encyclopaedia); [Culture Trip](https://theculturetrip.com/europe/switzerland/articles/meet-the-tatzelwurm-switzerlands-nightmarish-7-foot-lizard) (popular).
Sharing: **open** — European folklore with no living cult.

**The mountain dragon that dropped a healing stone** (`dragon_that_left_a_healing_stone`, creature) — Swiss legends of Mount Pilatus.
Dragons lived in Pilatus's caves; one in 1421 left a stone later credited with curing illness (tested as burnt clay in 2006).
Sources: [American Alpine Club, "Legends of Mount Pilatus"](https://publications.americanalpineclub.org/articles/12194928200/Legends-of-Mount-Pilatus) (popular, journal); [ausflug.blog](https://ausflug.blog/en/nature-landscape/1777-pilatus-the-dragon-mountain) (popular).
Sharing: **open** — Swiss folk legend. Details mostly from tourism retellings; the primary (Cysat, 1661) was not opened.

**The capricious lord of the mountain and its weather** (`lord_of_mountain_weather`, spirit) — Rübezahl / Krakonoš.
A shape-changing mountain spirit who sends fog and snow from a clear sky; kind to the honest poor, teaching medicine and giving gifts, merciless to mockers.
Sources: [Wikipedia, Rübezahl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%BCbezahl) (encyclopaedia); [Radio Prague International](https://english.radio.cz/node/8566039) (public broadcaster); [Geoheritage article, Springer 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12371-025-01110-8) (academic; not opened).
Sharing: **open** — regional folklore collected since the 17th century, a children's TV figure.

**The great bird of the far peak who raises and heals** (`great_healing_bird_of_the_peak`, creature) — Persian Simurgh (Shahnameh).
A vast mountain bird who raises the abandoned Zal, gives him feathers to summon her, teaches the birth that saves Rudaba and Rostam, and heals Rostam.
Sources: [Wikipedia, Simurgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simurgh) (encyclopaedia); [PMC article on the Shahnameh birth](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4154908) (academic); [World History Encyclopedia](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/1484/twelve-ancient-persian-mythological-creatures/) (encyclopaedia).
Sharing: **open** — classical published epic; not a worshipped being. Mount Alborz vs Qaf varies by source.

**The stranger on the high road who takes people's fat** (`stranger_who_steals_fat`, monster) — Andean pishtaco / kharisiri / ñakaq.
An outsider who waylays people on lonely paths to drain their fat; anthropologists read it as a figure of colonial and economic violence since the conquest.
Sources: [Wikipedia, Pishtaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pishtaco) (encyclopaedia); [Harvard ReVista](https://revista.drclas.harvard.edu/peruvian-pishtacos) (academic); [Folklore Thursday](https://folklorethursday.com/halloween/the-pishtaco-fat-stealing-ghoul-of-the-andes/) (popular).
Sharing: **open** — widely published, discussed openly in Peru. Sensitive: a racialised figure of real fear; never depict as an ethnic type.

**The winter hag who dropped the mountains from her apron** (`winter_hag_who_built_mountains`, spirit) — Scottish Gaelic Cailleach Bheur / Beira.
A winter and storm hag seated on Ben Nevis who built hills from rocks dropped from her apron and keeps spring captive until her season ends.
Sources: [John Muir Trust, Schiehallion and the Queen of Winter](https://www.johnmuirtrust.org/whats-new/news/1574-schiehallion-and-the-queen-of-winter) (community, conservation charity); [Inverness Courier](https://inverness-courier.co.uk/news/myth-explains-the-story-of-winter-and-spring-s-rebirth-261587) (regional press).
Sharing: **open** — Gaelic folklore of a dead religion. Tales vary by district; the Brìde captivity strand needs an academic check.

**The hooded spirit of the mine who demands silence and honesty** (`hooded_mine_spirit`, spirit) — German Bergmönch (Harz, Erzgebirge); related Cornish and Welsh knockers.
A giant hooded "mountain monk" in the mines who rewards good miners and punishes whistling, cursing, idleness and greed.
Sources: [Wikipedia, Bergmönch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergm%C3%B6nch) (encyclopaedia); [Encyclopedia.com, Mines, Haunted](https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/mines-haunted) (encyclopaedia).
Sharing: **open** — published legend (Grimm's *Deutsche Sagen*, Bechstein).

**Restricted, not coded (mountain):** Andean apus and Pachamama (living, worshipped earth and mountain beings with active offerings); Tibetan mountain deities, protectors and gandharva cult imagery (living religion); Saint Yared as a figure (venerated saint; only the bird motif is coded, as unclear); specific sirinu sites, rituals and pinkillu recordings (secret / said to be dangerous); Rocky Mountains Indigenous figures (no community source sharing them; songs and ceremonies commonly restricted). **Left out on sourcing or overlap:** ccoa, the Andean hail-cat (bestiary sources only); Ladin salvans and the Saligen (tourism sources only); tengu (its music is already the conifer row `empty_mountain_music`); Caucasian fændyr origin (not sourced).
