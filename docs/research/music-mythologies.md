# Music mythologies by landscape

Researched **2026-11-18** for `research-music-mythologies`, by biome: for each of the eight
game biomes (`docs/design/design-guide.md`, *Mythic regions*), the cultures whose homeland is
that landscape, anywhere in the world, and their music deities, spirits and creatures. Every
entry carries a sharing check (**open**, **restricted**, **unclear**) under the change's
rules: only openly shared stories, figures unnamed in the game, imagery similar to and never
copying its heritage. Game notes in each entry are ideas, kept apart from what the sources say.

Desk research by Claude (four parallel passes, one per pair of biomes). **It cannot speak for
any community**: entries marked open are the best reading of public sources, and many were
read as search excerpts rather than in full. Each part says which primary texts still need
checking. Nothing here is cleared for shipping until task 0.4 (community review) is settled.

## Findings that matter for design

- **The desert start's original inspiration is mostly restricted.** The Mojave and Southwest
  and Aboriginal Australia were the first setting, but: Kokopelli comes from a Hopi katsina
  (Hopi Cultural Preservation Office protocol); Anangu ask that the Uluru stories not be retold
  at all; Southern Paiute and Chemehuevi Salt Songs are for community use; Chumash rock art is
  under image sovereignty; Cahuilla bird songs are sung at funerals and must not be sampled.
  The open desert candidates come from the Arabian, Saharan and Mongolian deserts. The one
  Southwest-adjacent opening is the Kumeyaay *Tu cuk* story pattern (chaparral reserve), from
  a community singer's public text. A Mojave figure would need **community consultation**.
- **The pool leans European and Greek**, because published folklore clears easily and living
  Indigenous traditions often do not. Candidates that would balance it need consultation first:
  the Lakota and Dakota courting flute (open with care, told publicly by named storytellers),
  the Andean sirinu (spring spirits who tune instruments; the best fit of all, but unclear),
  and the Cherokee Little People (unclear).
- **Restricted, never used:** Mbuti molimo; Jurupari/Kowai flutes of the northwest Amazon;
  Haudenosaunee Jo-ga-oh; Anishinaabe Big Drum; Kwakwaka'wakw masks, Haida and Tlingit crests,
  Coast Salish songs; Sámi drums and personal joik; White Buffalo Calf Woman; Yolŋu songs;
  Mami Wata shrine practice; Gnawa mluk. **Living deities not to be shown as dormant carvings:**
  Àyàn Agalú, the Andean apus, Tibetan deities, Benzaiten's hidden images.
- **Many figures make instruments from the land** (a pike's jaw, a birch, a horse, a camel,
  reeds, an oak). That is the game's recipe model: a world asset yields an instrument.

## Part 1: Desert and chaparral


Researched 2026-11-18. Each figure is marked **open**, **restricted** or **unclear** following the
project's sharing rules. Source kinds: community / academic / museum / encyclopaedia / popular /
primary (ancient text). Game notes are ideas only and are kept separate from the research.


### 1. Desert (deserts and xeric shrublands, playas, endorheic basins)

**Cultures looked at:** Arabian and Saharan Bedouin and Arabic lexicography; Tuareg (Aïr, Acacus,
Tadrart); Aymara and Quechua of the Bolivian altiplano and puna (high cold desert, salt flats, next to
the Atacama); |Xam San (Karoo); Mongolian and Tuvan steppe-desert; Cahuilla, Southern Paiute and
Chemehuevi (Mojave and Colorado deserts); Hopi and Ancestral Puebloan (Kokopelli); Tohono O'odham
(I'itoi); Anangu (Uluru, Central Australia); Nama Khoekhoe (Heitsi-eibib cairns).

#### 1.1 'Azīf: the humming of the jinn in the empty desert (Arabian and Saharan deserts)

- **Tied to:** sound in the landscape (night hum, wind, booming dunes), sand dunes, waterless desert.
- **Summary:** Classical Arabic has a word, *'azīf*, for a low humming or drum-like sound heard at
  night in the desert. Desert people took it for the voice of the jinn. The same entry describes
  *'azīf al-raml*, "the sound of the sands", a noise from dunes whose cause is unknown. Booming or
  singing dunes (about 30 known sites, including the Rub' al Khali) are the physical phenomenon.
  North African writers and Marco Polo, in the Gobi, also credited them to spirits.
- **Sources:**
  - [Lane's Arabic-English Lexicon, vol. 5, root ع ز ف](https://lanelexicon.mirrorservice.org/Volume5/00000320.pdf) (academic: 19th-century lexicon built from classical Arabic dictionaries)
  - [Hawramani Arabic lexicon, عزف](https://arabiclexicon.hawramani.com/%d8%b9%d9%8e%d8%b2%d9%90%d9%81/) (academic, a lexicon mirror)
  - [The National (UAE), "Symphony of the sands"](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae-news/science/symphony-of-the-sands) (popular, regional press)
  - [Smithsonian Magazine, singing dunes](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/why-sand-dunes-deserts-around-world-sing-1-180957071/) (popular science)
- **Sharing check: open.** This is a word and a folk explanation recorded in published classical
  lexicography, not a ceremony or a story owned by one group. Jinn belong to Islam, a living
  religion, so treat the belief respectfully. The game should present the hum as a voice of the
  place, not as a theological claim and not as demons.
- **Imagery check:** There is no traditional figurative image of the jinn to copy, and depicting
  spiritual beings as figures is sensitive in many Muslim contexts. Abstract imagery is safe: wind
  ripples, the slip faces of dunes, a shimmer or hum made visible. Avoid crescent, Qur'anic or
  calligraphic motifs.
- **Game notes (ideas):** A dune or playa edge that hums when the wind blows. The unnamed spirit is
  the hum itself, perhaps shown in wind-carved ripples on rock. It fits the silent windstorm
  opening: the storm is silent until the land is woken, and then the sand starts to sing.

#### 1.2 Masters of the land and khöömei (Mongolian and Tuvan steppe and Gobi)

- **Tied to:** throat singing (khöömei), imitation of wind, water, animals and mountains; spirits
  who own the places (master-spirits); cairns (ovoo).
- **Summary:** Mongolian and Tuvan herders hold that mountains, springs and rivers have spirit
  masters. Throat singing imitates the sounds of the landscape, and singers use it to please the
  spirit of a place. Rain-seeking rites at sacred mountains and ovoo cairns include praise,
  whistled melodies and khöömii. Writers on the tradition describe the singing as giving back to
  nature what was borrowed from it.
- **Sources:**
  - [Britannica, "Throat singing"](https://www.britannica.com/art/throat-singing) (encyclopaedia)
  - [Smithsonian Folkways, khöömei and the natural world](https://folkways.si.edu/news-and-press/unesco-collection-week-12-the-natural-world-in-mongolia-and-beyond) (museum / archive)
  - [Theodore Levin and Michael Edgerton, *Scientific American* 1999, Tuvan throat singing](https://gauss.dartmouth.edu/~m5f10/Levin1999SciAm_tuvan_throat_singing.pdf) (academic)
  - [Landscape Music, "Sketches of Nature: Landscape Music in the Central Asian Steppe"](https://landscapemusic.org/essays/sketches-of-nature-landscape-music-in-the-central-asian-steppe/) (academic essay)
  - [De Gruyter journal article (2025, doi 10.1515/modi-2025-0005) on khöömii in rain rites at mountains and ovoo](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/modi-2025-0005/pdf) (academic)
  - [UNESCO, Mongolian traditional art of Khöömei](https://es.unesco.org/silkroad/node/855) (international heritage body; inscriptions are nominated by the state with community consent)
- **Sharing check: open** for the general idea (spirits of place, singing to them, khöömei as
  imitation of nature), which Mongolian and Tuvan performers explain publicly and which UNESCO
  lists as shared heritage. Particular shamanic invocations and named local spirits are
  **unclear**: leave them out.
- **Imagery check:** Ovoo cairns, blue khadag scarves and horsehead fiddles (morin khuur) are
  openly visible, but an ovoo is an active shrine. Do not show players kicking, looting or building
  a working ovoo. A generic stacked-stone cairn is safe. Shamanic costume and mirror (toli) belong
  to practitioners and should be avoided.
- **Game notes (ideas):** An unnamed master-spirit of a rock outcrop that answers when the player
  sounds a drone and its overtone. It fits the burbler's clock and a future harmonic or formant
  filter.

#### 1.3 Sereno (sirinu, serena): the spirit of springs and cliffs who gives melodies (Aymara and Quechua, Bolivian altiplano)

- **Tied to:** springs (*vertientes*), precipices, night; new melodies and verses each festival
  season; tuning instruments.
- **Summary:** In northern Potosí, Bolivia, the Sereno is a spirit of the lower world who lives in
  springs and on cliffs. Ritual specialists leave food and drink beside musicians' instruments in
  remote places. The Sereno then comes out, sings and makes the instruments sound, giving the
  community its new melodies. It is dual, man-woman (*kari-warmi*), and sometimes appears as a
  small man on a favoured musician's shoulder. The bond has to be renewed with fresh offerings.
- **Sources:**
  - [Luis Fernando Botero Villegas, *Gazeta de Antropología* 20 (2004), art. 13](https://www.ugr.es/~pwlac/G20_13LuisFernando_Botero_Villegas.html) (academic, summarising Walter Sánchez 1988)
  - [UAB research portal, "The dark world and the music in the Andean myths"](https://portalrecerca.uab.cat/ca/publications/the-dark-world-and-the-music-in-the-andean-myths/) (academic abstract: the sirinu or Serena as music hidden in springs)
  - [Journal of Music Archaeology (ÖAW), Andean instruments](https://jma.vlg.oeaw.ac.at/index.php/jma/article/download/4/7/824) (academic)
  - [Comboni Missionaries, "Bolivia. The Sound of the Andes"](https://combonimissionaries.ie/?p=8676) (popular, religious outreach)
- **Sharing check: unclear.** The figure is widely discussed in folklore writing, but the sources
  say only specialists may contact it, and the offering rite is a living practice. I found no
  community source on outsiders depicting it. Using a "spring that gives melodies" as a pattern is
  low risk. Reproducing the offering ritual is not.
- **Biome caveat:** The puna and altiplano are WWF "montane grasslands and shrublands", but they
  are cold, high deserts with salt flats (Uyuni) next to the Atacama. Placing the Sereno in the
  desert set is a judgement call.
- **Imagery check:** Bolivian colonial churches have carved sirens playing charangos (reported by
  ethnomusicologist Thomas Turino), and that siren-with-instrument image is colonial-Christian and
  public. Do not copy the man-woman figure or the small man on the shoulder from living accounts.
- **Game notes (ideas):** A spring in a dry basin where an instrument left overnight is found
  re-tuned, or where the player hears a melody they did not play. This maps closely onto a recipe
  book that learns new patterns. It is the strongest thematic fit in this set, which makes its
  unclear status worth resolving before use.

#### 1.4 !Khwa, the rain animal of the waterhole (|Xam San, Karoo)

- **Tied to:** waterholes and pans that fill and dry (endorheic), rain, thunder; rock paintings.
- **Summary:** In the |Xam accounts recorded by Bleek and Lloyd in the 1870s, the rain is an
  animal that lives in the waterhole and in fact is the water there. It also crosses the sky as a
  rain cloud, pictured as an eland bull with streams of rain for legs. There is a fierce rain-bull
  and a gentle rain-cow. Rain-makers led it out of the waterhole on a thong to bring rain. |Xam
  narrators identified some rock paintings as rain animals.
- **Sources:**
  - [Digital Bleek and Lloyd, "Rain power" (University of Cape Town)](https://digitalbleeklloyd.uct.ac.za/cgi-bin/view/Curations/curatorium.zip/curatorium/pages/rain-power.html) (museum / archive: |Xam narrators' words)
  - [J. M. de Prada-Samper, "Rain stories", *Folkloristics* 2019](https://folkloristics.org/pdf/2019/rain-stories-interpreting-water-beings-in-the-folklore-of-the-southern-african-khoisan-and-their-descendants.pdf) (academic)
  - [Cambridge, *Customs and Beliefs of the |Xam*, chapter "Rain"](https://cambridge.org/core/books/customs-and-beliefs-of-the-xam/rain/C339D2C4B41D5B98A295983833FC38AB) (academic)
- **Sharing check: unclear.** The accounts are public in a university archive. The |Xam language is
  no longer spoken, but San descendant communities are active, and the South African San Council's
  2017 Code of Research Ethics asks for consultation and benefit-sharing when San knowledge is used
  commercially ([GroundUp](https://groundup.org.za/article/san-council-launches-code-ethics-researchers/), popular news;
  [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/san-people-south-africa-issue-code-ethics-researchers-180962615/), popular).
  The code is about research and traditional knowledge, and I found nothing on stories or images
  in games. Ask the San Council before use. The music link is also weak: the sources tie the rain
  animal to rain-making, not to song or instruments.
- **Imagery check:** San rock art (eland, therianthropes, rain animals) is heavily reproduced, but
  it belongs to descendant communities, and South African heritage law protects it. Do not trace or
  copy paintings. A large, horned, cloud-legged shape is a generic idea, but close resemblance to
  known panels would not be.
- **Game notes (ideas):** A dry pan that a heartbeat (stream) wakes into a brief rain-shape. Use it
  only if the San Council agrees.

#### 1.5 Kel Essuf: people of the wild and solitude (Tuareg, Sahara: Aïr, Acacus, Tadrart, Djado)

- **Tied to:** empty desert places, solitude, nostalgia; healing drums and women's choruses;
  rock engravings.
- **Summary:** For the Tuareg, the Kel Essuf are spirits of *essuf*: the wild, solitude and
  longing, both a feeling and a place. They meet travellers in dark or remote places and can cause
  illness. Healing is a public musical exorcism, with a drummer, a chorus of women singers and a
  head dance. Separately, local Tuareg call one style of small, stylised human petroglyphs in the
  Acacus, Tadrart and Djado rock shelters "Kel Essuf", "people of the spirits".
- **Sources:**
  - [Susan J. Rasmussen, "Friends of the Kel Essuf", *Cultural Survival Quarterly* 27-2](https://www.culturalsurvival.org/node/3880) (academic, published in an Indigenous-rights quarterly)
  - [Rasmussen, *Oral Tradition* 13/2, tende n goumaten songs](https://journal.oraltradition.org/wp-content/uploads/files/articles/13ii/2_rasmussen.pdf) (academic)
  - [Round Head Catalogue, "Kel Essuf" petroglyphs (J. Soukopova)](https://roundheadsahara.com/rock-art/kel-essuf/) (academic catalogue)
  - [Smithsonian EEPA, photograph of a Tuareg women's tende](https://si.edu/object/archives/components/sova-eepa-1973-001-ref3484) (museum)
- **Sharing check: unclear.** The spirits and the exorcism are a living healing practice documented
  by an outside anthropologist. I found no Tuareg community statement on outsiders using them.
  Treat possession rites and their songs as not for use. Tende as social women's drumming is
  public.
- **Imagery check:** The petroglyphs are prehistoric (not Tuareg-made), published in open
  catalogues and protected World Heritage sites (Tadrart Acacus). The safe approach is a small
  oval-bodied stick-figure style, generically similar but not traced. Tuareg leatherwork and silver
  crosses belong to living artisans: do not copy them.
- **Game notes (ideas):** Engravings found in a rock shelter on the playa's edge, which come alive
  as a chorus-and-drum pattern. Frame this as longing for a lost place rather than as illness or
  possession.

#### 1.6 Bird songs (Cahuilla, Colorado Desert and Coachella Valley; also Serrano and Kumeyaay)

- **Tied to:** song cycles of about 300 songs, gourd rattles, migration across the land, birds.
- **Summary:** Cahuilla bird songs are a long cycle sung from dusk to dawn over several nights.
  Singers describe them as the people's migration and search for a homeland, and some tie them to
  the creation story. They are taught by elders and accompanied by rattles.
- **Sources:**
  - [Agua Caliente Cultural Museum, "Singing the Birds" (KESQ news report)](https://kesq.com/news/2018/01/27/agua-caliente-cultural-museum-presents-singing-the-birds/) (popular report on a community museum event)
  - [Joshua Tree NP, Native Americans](https://www.nps.gov/jotr/learn/photosmultimedia/native-americans.htm) (government)
  - [Native America Calling, bird songs](https://www.nativeamericacalling.com/friday-june-12-2015-bird-songs/) (community: Native-produced radio)
- **Sharing check: open to hear, not to reuse.** Bird singing is performed in public (Malki Museum's
  Kewet fiesta, Agua Caliente's annual event). The songs are also sung at funerals and belong to
  their singers and families. Do not record, sample or imitate the songs. There is no single deity
  here, so this is better as context than as a figure.
- **Imagery check:** Gourd rattles and the general idea of birds as messengers are openly shown.
  Specific basket designs belong to their makers and families.
- **Game notes (ideas):** Inspiration only, for example migrating birds that pass over a waking
  basin. Do not build a mechanic on Cahuilla song.

**Desert shortlist (strongest open candidates):**

1. **'Azīf, the humming of the empty desert and the singing dunes:** open, sound-native and
   landscape-native, and it needs no figure, which avoids the depiction problem.
2. **The master-spirit of a place, answered with drone and overtone (steppe-desert):** open as a
   concept, it rewards sound-making, and it fits a generic cairn or outcrop.
3. *(Pending a sharing check)* **The Sereno of the spring:** the best thematic fit (a spring gives
   melodies), but marked unclear.

**Desert rejections:**

- **Kokopelli / Kokopölö (Hopi, Ancestral Puebloan):** restricted. The name comes from a Hopi
  katsina (Kookopölö). Hopi say katsinam are living religion, and the Hopi Cultural Preservation
  Office has a protocol on the use of Hopi cultural expressions
  ([HCPO](https://www.hopi-nsn.gov/hopi-cultural-preservation-office/), community). The
  flute-player petroglyph is too entangled with the katsina to use safely, and popular use does
  not settle it.
- **Kuniya and Liru (Anangu, Uluru):** restricted. Parks Australia, on behalf of Anangu, asks that
  the stories not be retold in any part
  ([uluru.gov.au](https://uluru.gov.au/discover/culture/stories/kuniya-liru-stories/), government
  and community co-managed).
- **Salt Songs (Southern Paiute / Nuwuvi, Chemehuevi):** restricted. The project keeps the songs
  for Nuwuvi use only and retains all cultural property rights
  ([Native Land / Salt Song Trail](https://nativeland.org/salt-song-trail), community project).
- **I'itoi / Man in the Maze (O'odham):** a used emblem tied to sacred Baboquivari, and not a
  music figure.
- **Heitsi-eibib (Nama Khoekhoe):** has cairns, but no music tie was found.


### 2. Chaparral / Mediterranean scrub

**Cultures looked at:** ancient Greek (Arcadia, Boeotia, Thrace, Phrygia in Anatolia); Bronze Age
Levant (Ugarit); Kumeyaay and Chumash (California chaparral); Gnawa (Atlantic Moroccan coast,
Essaouira); Khoekhoe (Cape fynbos: nothing music-specific found); Noongar (SW Australia: nothing
music-specific checked).

**Ancient Greek sharing note, for all Greek entries:** the ancient religion is known from published
classical literature, so its stories are open. A small living revival exists: Hellenic Polytheism,
recognised in Greece as a "known religion" in 2017 through YSEE
([Greek Reporter](https://greekreporter.com/2017/04/20/greek-state-recognizes-paganism-as-religion/), popular news;
[Wild Hunt](https://wildhunt.org/2017/04/greek-paganism-legally-recognized-as-known-religion-in-greece.html), popular / pagan press).
That revival is no reason to avoid these figures, but avoid mocking them or showing worship rites.

#### 2.1 Syrinx: the nymph who became reeds (Greek, Arcadia, river Ladon)

- **Tied to:** reeds, river banks, panpipes, wind through hollow stems.
- **Summary:** Syrinx, a naiad of the Arcadian river Ladon, fled Pan and was turned into water
  reeds at the river's edge. Pan heard the wind sigh in them, cut the reeds and joined them into
  the first panpipes, which took her name. Longus tells it differently: she hides among the reeds
  and vanishes into the marsh.
- **Sources:**
  - [Theoi, Syrinx (quoting Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 1, and Longus)](https://theoi.com/Nymphe/NympheSyrinx.html) (encyclopaedia quoting primary texts)
  - [EBSCO Research Starters, Pan and Syrinx](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/literature-and-writing/pan-and-syrinx) (encyclopaedia)
- **Sharing check: open** (dead religion, classical literature; see the Greek note above). The
  pursuit is a story of attempted assault: show the transformation and the reeds, not the chase.
- **Imagery check:** Reeds, a river bank and stepped pipes of graded lengths are generic and
  public. Avoid copying specific Greek vase compositions or classical sculpture as identifiable
  Pan figures.
- **Game notes (ideas):** Reeds are already the game's stream ingredient. An unnamed spirit in a
  reed bed whose stems sound when the wind passes, and harvested reeds becoming the first pipe or
  oscillator, fit the existing reed loop closely.

#### 2.2 Echo: the voice scattered into the rocks (Greek, Mount Kithairon; Longus 3.23)

- **Tied to:** echo, rocks, caves, hillsides, singing and dance.
- **Summary:** Echo is a mountain nymph of Kithairon, a singer and dancer. In Ovid she wastes away
  for Narcissus until only her voice and bones remain, and the bones become stone. In Longus'
  *Daphnis and Chloe* (3.23), Pan, jealous of her music, maddens shepherds who scatter her still
  singing body across the earth, which hides it. Ever since, the land answers every sound.
- **Sources:**
  - [Theoi, Echo (Ovid *Met.* 3; Homeric Hymn 19)](https://www.theoi.com/Nymphe/NympheEkho.html) (encyclopaedia quoting primary texts)
  - [Longus, *Daphnis and Chloe*, ToposText](https://topostext.org/work/204) (primary text in translation; see 3.23)
  - [Boston College Latin commentary, on the Pan and Echo myth](https://sites.bc.edu/latin-commentary/?p=2945) (academic: calls Longus 3.23 the fullest surviving version)
  - [Wikipedia, Echo (mythology)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echo_(mythology)) (encyclopaedia)
- **Sharing check: open** (classical literature). Longus' version is violent: imply it rather than
  show it.
- **Imagery check:** Caves, rock faces and a voice without a body are generic. Do not show a
  dismembered figure.
- **Game notes (ideas):** This is the best mythic fit for "carved into rocks". Echo's voice lies in
  the rocks, so the echo grotto already in the game could hold an unnamed singing-rock spirit that
  repeats the last notes played to it (a delay module discovered in the world).

#### 2.3 The cicadas of the Muses (Greek, Plato's *Phaedrus* 258e-259d)

- **Tied to:** cicadas, summer heat, the noon drone in scrub and plane trees; the Muses.
- **Summary:** Socrates tells Phaedrus that cicadas were once men, living before the Muses were
  born. When song first appeared, some loved it so much that they sang without eating or drinking
  until they died. The Muses made them cicadas, who need no food and sing from birth to death. They
  then report to each Muse which humans honour her art.
- **Sources:**
  - [Plato, *Phaedrus* 259, Fowler translation (Perseus/Perseids)](https://cts.perseids.org/read/greekLit/tlg0059/tlg012/perseus-eng2/259) (primary text)
  - [Andrea Capra, *Plato's Four Muses* (Center for Hellenic Studies)](https://chs.harvard.edu/chapter/chapter-4-the-muses-and-the-tree/) (academic)
  - [Wikipedia, Cicadas in mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cicadas_in_mythology) (encyclopaedia)
- **Sharing check: open** (classical literature).
- **Imagery check:** Cicadas are generic natural imagery. The golden cicada hair ornaments of
  archaic Athens are a public museum motif. The safest route is a stylised insect.
- **Game notes (ideas):** A creature resource like the lightning bug: cicadas in scrub oak that
  drone at noon, perhaps a noise or drone waveform source. The myth's "they report who honours the
  music" maps onto the goals hub.

#### 2.4 Orpheus, whom trees and stones followed (Greek, Thrace; Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 10-11)

- **Tied to:** lyre and song; trees, rocks and animals moved by music.
- **Summary:** Orpheus' playing was so powerful that trees gathered around him in a bare place to
  give it shade, and wild animals and even stones followed him. Ovid gives a catalogue of the trees
  that came. After his death, his head and lyre floated singing down the river Hebrus to Lesbos.
- **Sources:**
  - [Theoi, Ovid *Metamorphoses* 11](https://www.theoi.com/Text/OvidMetamorphoses11.html) (primary text in translation)
  - [Poetry in Translation, Ovid *Metamorphoses* 10, "The gathering of the trees" (10.86-105)](https://www.poetryintranslation.com/PITBR/Latin/Metamorph10.php) (primary text in translation)
- **Sharing check: open** (classical literature). Orphism was a mystery cult with initiate texts,
  but the narrative myth is public literature. Do not use the Orphic gold tablets or hymns as ritual
  scripts.
- **Biome note:** Thrace is temperate, not true Mediterranean scrub, but the myth is pan-Greek, and
  "music gathers trees onto a bare hill" fits the scrub-greening theme.
- **Imagery check:** A lyre and animals around a seated singer is a long public tradition, from
  Roman mosaics onward. Keep the spirit unnamed and avoid copying a specific mosaic.
- **Game notes (ideas):** The mythic template for "music grows foliage": trees drawn in around a
  sounding fire, or an unnamed lyre-spirit carved into an oak that is woken by song.

#### 2.5 Kinnaru: the god who was a lyre (Ugarit, Levantine coast, Bronze Age)

- **Tied to:** a stringed instrument (lyre), deified.
- **Summary:** Kinnaru was a god of Ugarit, on the Syrian coast, who was most likely the lyre
  itself made divine. He appears only in god lists and offering lists, with no known myth. Scholars
  link him, with debate, to Kinyras, the musician king of Cyprus.
- **Sources:**
  - [John C. Franklin, *Kinyras: The Divine Lyre*, ch. 7 "Kinnaru of Ugarit" (Center for Hellenic Studies)](https://www-current.chs.harvard.edu/chapter/7-kinnaru-of-ugarit) (academic)
  - [Wikipedia, Kinyras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinyras) (encyclopaedia)
- **Sharing check: open.** A dead Bronze Age religion with no living descendant tradition, known
  from excavated texts.
- **Imagery check:** No cult image of Kinnaru is known, so there is nothing to copy. Generic
  Levantine lyres are attested in public archaeology.
- **Game notes (ideas):** "The instrument is the god" fits the rule that every machine is an
  instrument. An unnamed spirit could live in a found lyre-shaped rock or branch fork that becomes
  a string-voice module.

#### 2.6 Tu cuk and the songs given to the people (Kumeyaay, San Diego chaparral to the Colorado Desert)

- **Tied to:** bird songs, gourd and tortoise-shell rattles, birds as messengers, the coast and the
  hills.
- **Summary:** In a public telling by Kumeyaay bird singer Ron Christman, the Creator gave the bird
  songs to the people. A bright, jealous bird tried to keep and sell them, and was stripped of his
  colour and voice, becoming the crow, which can only squawk. A humble little bird, Tu cuk, carried
  the songs to the people instead, and the Creator promised it would always be remembered. The
  songs teach respect for people and all living things. They are accompanied by rattles, not drums.
- **Sources:**
  - [Kumeyaay.com, "Traditional Origins of Southern CA Bird Songs", public performance and text by Ron Christman (Ipai/Tipai)](https://www.kumeyaay.com/traditional-origins-of-southern-ca-bird-songs.html) (community: a Kumeyaay information site, text by a community singer)
  - [Smithsonian Folklife Festival, the Kumeyaay communities of California](https://festival.si.edu/blog/2016/the-kumeyaay-communities-of-california/) (museum)
- **Sharing check: open (the story), with care.** A community member published it as a public
  performance text, and the story itself argues for sharing. The songs themselves are also sung at
  funerals and reburials, so **do not reproduce, sample or imitate them**. Use the moral pattern (a
  small humble bird brings song; the hoarder loses his voice), not the songs.
- **Imagery check:** A small bird and a dark crow are generic. Rattles are shown openly in public
  performance. Avoid specific Kumeyaay basketry or regalia designs.
- **Game notes (ideas):** An unnamed small bird in scrub oak whose call becomes a pattern only once
  the player shares (places or gives away) a sound. It is a natural counterweight to hoarding in
  the inventory.

#### 2.7 Marsyas: the satyr of the aulos whose blood became a river (Greek, Celaenae in Phrygia)

- **Tied to:** the aulos (double reed pipe), a river's source, a contest with Apollo.
- **Summary:** Marsyas found the aulos that Athena threw away and became so skilled that he
  challenged Apollo. He lost, and was flayed in a cave near Celaenae. The river Marsyas springs from
  his blood, or from the tears of those who mourned him. His pipes floated down the Maeander.
- **Sources:**
  - [Theoi, Marsyas](https://theoi.com/Georgikos/SatyrosMarsyas.html) (encyclopaedia quoting primary texts)
  - [Wikipedia, Marsyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsyas) (encyclopaedia)
- **Sharing check: open** (classical literature), but the story is a graphic punishment.
- **Biome note:** Celaenae lies in the Anatolian interior, semi-arid rather than coastal scrub.
- **Imagery check:** Do not depict the flaying. A pipe found in a spring is generic.
- **Game notes (ideas):** Weaker than the others: a spring that yields a reed-pipe voice. Use it
  only if a double-reed waveform is needed.

**Chaparral shortlist (strongest open candidates):**

1. **Syrinx / the singing reed bed:** open, and it fits directly onto the game's existing reed
   resource and the stream.
2. **Echo / the voice in the rocks:** open, and it fits the echo grotto and "found in rocks". A
   delay or echo module is a natural discovery.
3. **The Muses' cicadas:** open, a creature resource for scrub oak, and summer drone as sound.
   (Close runners-up: Kinnaru, the deified lyre, and Tu cuk, from a community source.)

**Chaparral rejections:**

- **Chumash rock art (Painted Cave and others):** restricted / unclear. The Santa Ynez Band asked
  for a film about the paintings to be removed, citing image sovereignty
  ([Arizona AIFG](https://aifg.arizona.edu/node/160), academic), and rock-art site locations are
  withheld.
- **Gnawa mluk (Moroccan coast):** restricted. The spirits are invoked in the lila, a private
  all-night healing and possession ceremony. Only festival excerpts are public.
- **Pan:** not rejected but folded in. He is open, yet his main music myths are the Syrinx and Echo
  stories, which are better used as places than as a goat-god figure.
- **Khoekhoe (fynbos) and Noongar (SW Australia):** no openly shared music figure was found in the
  time available. Not invented.

## Part 2: Broadleaf forest and conifer rainforest


Research for Stubby Music's mythic music figures, sorted by landscape. Compiled 2026-11-18 from web
searches. **Source kinds:** community, academic, museum, encyclopaedia, popular, primary text.
**Sharing:** open, restricted or unclear. Game notes are ideas only, kept apart from the research.

Caveat: most sources here were read only as search excerpts. A few primary texts (CELT, the
Kalevala translations, Mooney) should be opened and checked before anything ships.


### 1. Broadleaf forest (temperate broadleaf and mixed)

**Cultures looked at:** Irish (Tuatha Dé Danann myth; fairy-fort folklore), Welsh (Mabinogi),
East Slavic (leshy), Japanese (kodama, yamabiko), Cherokee (Yunwi Tsunsdi), Haudenosaunee
(Jo-ga-oh), Anishinaabe (the drum). I checked Baltic and Korean figures only briefly and found
nothing I could source well enough to include.

#### 1.1 The Dagda's harp (Uaithne, "Daur Dá Bláo", the oak of two meadows): Irish, early medieval texts
- **Tied to:** a harp, an oak (one of the harp's names), and three kinds of music: sorrow, joy and sleep.
- **Summary:** In *Cath Maige Tuired* the Fomorians take the Dagda's harp. He calls it by its names,
  one of which is *Daur Dá Bláo*, usually read as "oak of two meadows". The harp flies off the wall
  to him, and he plays the three strains: sorrow (*goltraí*), laughter (*geantraí*) and sleep
  (*suantraí*), which sends the enemy to sleep. It is a living instrument that answers when called by name.
- **Sources:**
  - [CELT edition of *Cath Maige Tuired*](https://celt.ucc.ie/published/T300010/text165.html) (primary text, University College Cork; academic)
  - [Uaithne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uaithne) (encyclopaedia; notes disputed readings of the names)
  - [earlygaelicharp.info: mythic harps](https://earlygaelicharp.info/tradition/mythic.htm) (specialist scholarly site)
- **Sharing:** **open**. This is medieval literature, published and edited for more than a century. It
  is not a living ceremony of a closed group. Modern Irish pagan groups use the figure, but that does
  not restrict it.
- **Imagery check:** **open:** an oak-wood harp, oak leaves, interlace in the general insular style.
  Do not copy a specific historic harp, such as the Trinity College harp, which is a national emblem
  and a trademarked state symbol.
- **Game notes (ideas):** an old oak, its trunk carved with a harp shape, that wakes when the player
  plays to it. Its three moods (sorrow, joy, sleep) could map to three settings or three kinds of response.

#### 1.2 Fairy music of the raths (ceol sí, the aos sí): Irish, living folklore
- **Tied to:** music heard from ringforts and fairy hawthorns, dancing, "low sweet strains", and
  fiddlers who learn tunes from it.
- **Summary:** Hundreds of 1930s accounts tell of music heard coming out of raths and forts, often
  at night. People who follow it find dancers inside. A fiddler hears it and plays along. A man
  cutting bushes in a rath hears music all around him. The music belongs to the place, and people
  warn against disturbing the fort or its thorn bushes.
- **Sources (all community: the Schools' Collection, written down by Irish schoolchildren from
  their own families, CC BY 4.0):**
  - [Edraguil, Monaghan](https://duchas.ie/en/cbes/4723853/4719152/4744434): "fairy music often heard … in low sweet strains"
  - [Urlingford, Kilkenny](https://duchas.ie/en/cbes/4742040/4730169/5018816): a man cutting bushes in a rath hears music all around him
  - [Baile Locha Riabhach, Galway](https://duchas.ie/en/cbes/4583354/4582357/4592354): a fiddler plays along with music from a fort
  - [Béal Trágha, Donegal](https://duchas.ie/en/cbes/4493798/4421707)
- **Sharing:** **open**. The community gave these accounts to a public archive that is openly
  licensed. One living sensitivity: many rural people still respect fairy forts and thorns and will
  not cut or disturb them. Do not show desecrating a fort as a game action.
- **Imagery check:** **open:** a ring of earth bank, a lone hawthorn, lights and dancing seen from
  outside. Avoid a twee "Tinker Bell" fairy look. The aos sí in these accounts are people-sized and uncanny.
- **Game notes (ideas):** a ring of earth around a thorn tree. When woken, faint dance music comes
  from inside and draws the player in, a first "follow it by ear" moment like the lightning bug's.

#### 1.3 The leshy's voice in the woods: East Slavic, folklore
- **Tied to:** the forest itself; laughing, whistling, singing and echoes heard in the woods.
- **Summary:** The leshy is master of the forest and its animals. People rarely see him, but they
  hear him laughing, whistling or singing among the trees, and he leads travellers astray. Folk
  belief treats whistling, calling out or mocking an echo in the forest as inviting him.
- **Sources:**
  - [Britannica, "leshy"](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/337093) (encyclopaedia)
  - Eastern Slavic demonology outline on Scribd (provenance unclear; not linked as authoritative).
    The echo and whistling taboo needs a scholarly source; W. F. Ryan's *The Bathhouse at Midnight*
    is a likely one, but I have not checked it.
- **Sharing:** **open**. This is folklore published in nineteenth- and twentieth-century ethnographic
  collections, not a closed tradition. Modern Slavic neopagans honour him, which does not restrict use.
- **Imagery check:** **open:** a moss-and-bark figure who changes height with the trees, hair and
  beard like lichen. Avoid the look of well-known recent game designs (for example *The Witcher*'s leshen).
- **Game notes (ideas):** a stand of trees whose echo is wrong: it answers late, in a different voice
  or with laughter. Waking it could turn that echo into a reliable delay or reverb source, which fits
  the echo-grotto acoustic work.

#### 1.4 Kodama and yamabiko: the tree spirit that answers: Japanese, folklore
- **Tied to:** old trees, the echo (*kodama* is also a word for echo), and mountain valleys.
- **Summary:** Kodama are spirits that live in trees. An echo in the woods was taken as the tree
  spirit answering, and yamabiko is the mountain spirit that calls back. Toriyama Sekien's Edo-period
  catalogue (1776) draws the kodama as an old man and woman beside a tree. Folklore also says cutting
  a tree a kodama lives in brings misfortune.
- **Sources:**
  - [Kodama (spirit)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodama_(spirit)) (encyclopaedia)
  - [Yamabiko (folklore)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamabiko_(folklore)) (encyclopaedia)
  - [yokai.jp: Kodama](https://yokai.jp/en/yokai/kodama) (popular)
  - [Wa-Raku, yamabiko and kodama](https://intojapanwaraku.com/culture/320863/) (popular, Japanese)
  - The scholarly source to check next is Nichibunken's Kaii–Yōkai Denshō database. My search found
    no entries from it to cite.
- **Sharing:** **open**. Published Edo folklore. Living sensitivity: in present-day Shinto, sacred
  trees (*shinboku*) at shrines are marked with a rope (*shimenawa*) and paper streamers (*shide*).
  Those are religious objects in current worship, so do not make them game props.
- **Imagery check:** **open:** Sekien's general idea of a figure beside an old tree; small pale
  forms in a forest. **Avoid** Studio Ghibli's *Princess Mononoke* kodama (white bobble-headed
  figures, which are copyrighted and instantly recognisable) and shrine regalia.
- **Game notes (ideas):** the simplest mechanic of any entry here: a carved tree that sings back
  what the player plays, as a delayed echo.

#### 1.5 Yunwi Tsunsdi (the Little People): Cherokee, southern Appalachian forest
- **Tied to:** drumming and dancing heard deep in the mountain forests and hills.
- **Summary:** Cherokee tradition tells of small people of the rocks and forests who love music
  and spend much of their time drumming and dancing. People say that if you go deep into the
  mountains and listen, you can hear their drums. They help people, including children and anyone
  lost, but they are easily offended and are not to be followed or bothered.
- **Sources:**
  - James Mooney, *Myths of the Cherokee* (1900, Bureau of American Ethnology; academic/ethnographic,
    collected from Cherokee informants), quoted in
    [Tryon Daily Bulletin](https://www.tryondailybulletin.com/?p=430685) (popular)
  - Lynn King Lossiah (Eastern Band Cherokee author), *The Secrets and Mysteries of the Cherokee Little
    People*, sold by the [Museum of the Cherokee People](https://museumstore.motcp.org/products/the-secrets-and-mysteries-of-the-cherokee-little-people)
    (community-authored and published; I have not read it)
- **Sharing:** **unclear, leaning open.** A Cherokee author has published these stories for general
  readers, and a tribal museum sells the book. Neither source I saw says how the stories may be
  used. The Little People are part of living belief, not only old folklore. Use them only as an
  unnamed drumming in the hills, and ask the Museum of the Cherokee People or EBCI before going further.
- **Imagery check:** **unclear:** I found no openly shared visual convention. Do not depict them.
  Hearing them without seeing them matches the stories anyway: drums heard far off, never a figure.
- **Game notes (ideas):** a drum rhythm heard from far up a wooded slope that the player can never
  reach. It may be better left unwoken: the stories say not to follow it.

#### 1.6 Jo-ga-oh and the Dark Dance: Haudenosaunee
- **Tied to:** little people of the woods, rocks and streams; a night ceremony with songs.
- **Summary:** Haudenosaunee tradition has little people, the jo-ga-oh. A medicine society (the
  Little People or Dark Dance society) keeps up relations with them through ceremonies held in the
  dark, with songs, to heal.
- **Sources:**
  - [Encyclopedia.com, "Iroquois Religious Traditions"](https://encyclopedia.com/environment/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/iroquois-religious-traditions) (encyclopaedia)
  - [A. C. Parker on the Dark ceremony, via sacred-texts](https://sacred-texts.com/nam/iro/parker/cohl151.htm)
    (early twentieth-century ethnography by Parker, who was of Seneca descent)
- **Sharing:** **restricted.** It is a medicine society: membership generally goes to people the
  society has cured, and the songs and rite belong to it. Do not use it.
- **Imagery check:** **restricted.** Do not depict it.
- **Game notes:** none. Listed so it is not picked up later by mistake.

#### 1.7 The Birds of Rhiannon (Adar Rhiannon): Welsh, Mabinogi
- **Tied to:** birdsong: "they wake the dead and lull the living to sleep".
- **Summary:** In the Second Branch of the Mabinogi, survivors returning from Ireland hear Rhiannon's
  three birds singing far out over the sea. Their song holds the feasters outside time for seven
  years. In *Culhwch and Olwen* the giant demands the birds as one of his tasks.
- **Sources:**
  - [Adar Rhiannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adar_Rhiannon) (encyclopaedia; links to translations)
  - [ynysprydein.org](https://ynysprydein.org/tag/culhwch/) (popular, Brythonic pagan)
- **Sharing:** **open.** Medieval Welsh literature.
- **Imagery check:** **open:** three small birds.
- **Landscape fit:** weak. The setting is the sea and Harlech, not a forest. Kept as a birdsong
  candidate only.
- **Game notes (ideas):** three birds in a woodland canopy whose song slows time, perhaps linked to
  the master clock or speed keys. They could fit coast or wetland better.

#### Broadleaf shortlist (open)
1. **Kodama / yamabiko: the answering tree.** Open, about a landscape (old trees, echoes), easy to
   make generic, and a clean mechanic (echo/delay). Keep it away from the Ghibli design and shrine regalia.
2. **The Dagda's oak harp.** Open, has a primary text, a tree link through the harp's oak name, and
   three musical moods.
3. Runner-up: **fairy music of the raths** (community-sourced and openly licensed, about a place). The
   leshy's echo is a strong alternative once a better source is found.


### 2. Conifer rainforest and boreal forest (temperate coniferous and taiga)

**Cultures looked at:** Finnish and Karelian (Kalevala: Väinämöinen, Tapio, Mielikki), Norwegian and
Swedish (fossegrim/strömkarl/näcken, huldra), Sámi (the drum, joik), Pacific Northwest Coast
(Kwakwaka'wakw Dzunuk'wa and Echo, Coast Salish song protocols), Japanese mountain cedar forest
(tengu-bayashi, yamabiko). I did not research Evenki or Khanty: I found no community source on
sharing, and their shamanic drums are by nature ceremonial.

#### 2.1 Väinämöinen's birch kantele: Finnish and Karelian, Kalevala
- **Tied to:** a birch tree turned into an instrument; the whole forest and its creatures come to listen.
- **Summary:** The first kantele, made of pike bone, is lost at sea (Runo 40–41). In Runo 44
  Väinämöinen walks the forest and hears a birch weeping that people only strip its bark and cut
  its branches. He makes his new kantele from it. When he plays, all of nature gathers to listen and
  is moved. (The detail of oak pegs and strings from a maiden's hair needs checking in the text; the
  Kirby/Crawford translations are the place to confirm it.)
- **Sources:**
  - [Crawford translation, Rune 44](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/elias-lonnrot/the-kalevala/john-martin-crawford/text/chapter-44) (primary text, public domain)
  - [Kirby translation](https://gutenberg.org/files/25953/25953-h/25953-h.htm) (primary text)
  - [Kalevala Society: what happens in the Kalevala](https://kalevalaseura.fi/en/what-happens-in-the-kalevala/)
    (Finnish cultural society; the closest thing to a community source here)
- **Sharing:** **open.** Lönnrot's epic (1835/1849) comes from Karelian and Finnish folk poetry
  sung by named rune singers, and is national literature published openly. Living sensitivity: a
  few Karelian communities in Russia are minority heritage holders, but the Kalevala itself is not restricted.
- **Imagery check:** **open:** a birch-bodied kantele, birch bark, a forest of listening animals.
  Avoid copying Akseli Gallen-Kallela's paintings directly (they are public domain but iconic; a pastiche
  reads as a copy).
- **Game notes (ideas):** the best fit in this biome: a weeping birch that becomes music. A birch
  carved with string lines that the player "restrings" by playing to it, after which creatures gather.

#### 2.2 Tapio and Mielikki's forest: horn and honey-flute: Finnish, Kalevala and charm poetry
- **Tied to:** the forest's god and mistress; the shepherd's horn and a "honey-flute" played to call
  the forest's goodwill.
- **Summary:** Tapio is the forest god and Mielikki the forest mistress, with their children
  Tellervo and Nyyrikki. In Rune 32 the herding charm asks the forest maidens for a shepherd's horn
  and Ukko's honey-flute, to play "the music of creation" so the forest will be kind to the herd.
  It is a sung bargain with the forest.
- **Sources:**
  - [Crawford translation, Rune 32](https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/elias-lonnrot/the-kalevala/john-martin-crawford/text/chapter-32) (primary text)
  - The best scholarly source for Tapio in charm poetry would be the Finnish Literature Society
    (SKS) or Anna-Leena Siikala's *Itämerensuomalaisten mytologia*; I have not checked either.
- **Sharing:** **open** (published folk poetry). Some modern Finnish pagans (Suomenusko) honour Tapio,
  which does not restrict use.
- **Imagery check:** **open:** a spruce-and-moss figure, a birch-bark horn, honey.
- **Game notes (ideas):** a mossy spruce grove that answers a horn call. The flute is Ukko's in the
  text, so use the horn for the forest.

#### 2.3 The fossegrim / strömkarl: the fiddler under the waterfall: Norwegian and Swedish folklore
- **Tied to:** a waterfall or mill-race in the forest; fiddle (the Hardanger fiddle in Norway); a teacher of music.
- **Summary:** A spirit who lives under waterfalls and mill-races and plays the fiddle. Fiddlers
  who bring him an offering, traditionally meat, are taught to play beyond human skill. Folklore says
  the master fiddler Myllarguten learned from him, and there is a well-known Hardanger tune called "Fossegrimen".
- **Sources:**
  - [Fossegrim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossegrim) (encyclopaedia)
  - ["The Fiddler and the Waterfall: The Quest for Inspiration in Norwegian Legends of Fiddlers", *Scottish Studies*](https://open.journals.ed.ac.uk/ScottishStudies/article/view/716) (academic, open access)
- **Sharing:** **open.** Folklore published in Norwegian and Swedish collections. The tunes are
  folk repertoire; any particular recording belongs to its performer.
- **Imagery check:** **open:** a figure in falling water, a fiddle. The Hardanger fiddle's decoration
  (inlay, a carved dragon or head scroll) is folk-craft style, not owned, but avoid copying a specific
  instrument. The Swedish *Näcken* also has a well-known nude painting (Josephson) that should not be imitated.
- **Game notes (ideas):** fits the mossy stream perfectly and ties in with streams and their filters.
  A waterfall on a stream, carved with a fiddler, that teaches a new module or tuning in return for an offering.

#### 2.4 The huldra and the herding call: Norwegian and Swedish folklore
- **Tied to:** summer mountain farms (*seter*) at the forest edge; herding calls (*kulokk*, kulning);
  music that lures people.
- **Summary:** The huldra is a beautiful forest woman with a cow's tail who visits summer farms and
  watches over cattle in the mountains. The herding calls herders used are tied to her in folklore,
  and her own singing lures people away.
- **Sources:**
  - [Smithsonian Folklife: kulning, Swedish herding calls](https://folklife.si.edu/magazine/kulning-swedish-herding-calls) (museum)
  - [Lokk (herding calls)](https://folkemusikk.custompublish.com/lokk-herding-calls.4610026-137785.html) (folk-music organisation page; not verified)
  - [Life in Norway: kulning](https://www.lifeinnorway.net/kulning-nordic-cow-singing/) (popular)
- **Sharing:** **open.** Published folklore; kulning is a living public vocal tradition that is taught openly.
- **Imagery check:** **open:** a woman of the woods glimpsed from behind, with bark or a hollow back
  and a tail. Avoid sexualised depictions, which are common in popular art but not needed.
- **Game notes (ideas):** a high call heard at the forest edge. Waking her could be the source of a
  sung-call module (a long, high glide).

#### 2.5 Tengu-bayashi and yamabayashi: music from the empty mountain: Japanese, mountain cedar forest
- **Tied to:** deep forested mountains, flute and drum music with no source, and the sound of trees
  falling where none fall.
- **Summary:** People reported festival music (flute and drum) from deep mountains where no one
  lives, and it seemed to move away as they came near. It was blamed on tengu (or on tanuki in the
  lowlands, as in the Honjo "baka-bayashi"). Tengu were also said to beat a drum inside trees and to
  make the sound of trees being felled in the night.
- **Sources:**
  - [Tanuki-bayashi](https://www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanuki-bayashi) (encyclopaedia)
  - [yokai.jp: tanuki-bayashi](https://yokai.jp/en/yokai/tanuki-bayashi) (popular)
  - [Matthew Meyer, "Tengu daoshi"](https://matthewmeyer.net/?p=33753) (popular, yōkai author)
  - Shizuoka *yamabayashi* and Tateyama tengu drumming, as reported in popular yōkai pages. These
    need a better source; Nichibunken's Kaii–Yōkai database is the one to check.
- **Sharing:** **open.** Edo and folk ghost lore. Living sensitivity: tengu are also worshipped at
  some mountain shrines (for example Kurama), and yamabushi mountain asceticism is a living religious
  practice. Do not copy yamabushi ritual dress or conch (*horagai*) ritual.
- **Imagery check:** **open:** a hidden band of drums and flutes, lanterns among cedars, feathers.
  The long-nosed red tengu mask is a common folk image but strongly tied to shrine festivals. The
  unnamed-spirit approach suggests using only the sound, or a feathered, bird-like hint.
- **Game notes (ideas):** a festival rhythm in a cedar grove that is always just out of reach, like
  the Cherokee drums but open. It could be a hidden sequencer pattern the player tracks down.

#### 2.6 Dzunuk'wa (Tsonoqua), wild woman of the woods; and the Echo mask: Kwakwaka'wakw, Pacific Northwest
- **Tied to:** deep cedar forest; her cry "huu huu"; Echo, a forest spirit that imitates every creature's voice.
- **Summary:** Dzunuk'wa is a giant woman of the woods whose cry echoes through the forest. She is
  dangerous to children but also gives wealth and the Water of Life to those who befriend her. Echo
  is shown in winter ceremonies with a mask whose mouthpieces are changed to imitate different creatures.
- **Sources:**
  - [Brooklyn Museum: Dzunuk'wa mask](https://www.brooklynmuseum.org/objects/154394) (museum: the dance "continues today by an individual who inherits the privilege")
  - [Reciprocal Research Network: Echo mask and mouthpieces](https://www.rrncommunity.org/items/560589/print) (museum and community research network)
  - [U'mista Cultural Centre](https://www.umista.ca/) (community; check its pages before any use)
- **Sharing:** **restricted.** The masks, dances and their stories are inherited privileges held by
  particular families and passed on at potlatch. The Echo mask was "reserved for certain individuals".
  Popular images of Tsonoqua do not make the figure open.
- **Imagery check:** **restricted** for both figures and their masks (round pursed mouth, sunken eyes,
  interchangeable mouthpieces). Northwest Coast **formline** as a general style is shared and taught,
  but in a game it would read as Northwest Coast specifically. Do not use it without a Northwest Coast
  artist and community partner.
- **Game notes:** do not use. An echo spirit is better done through kodama/yamabiko (open).

#### 2.7 The Sámi drum (goavddis) and joik: Sámi
- **Tied to:** the noaidi's drum (painted skin, a pointer that moves over the figures); joik, which
  sings a person, animal or place rather than about it.
- **Summary:** Sámi ceremonial drums were confiscated and burned in seventeenth-century witch trials.
  The Sámi Parliament now leads their return (for example Anders Poulsen's drum came back to Karasjok
  in 2022). Joik is living music; a personal joik belongs to the person it was made for.
- **Sources:**
  - [Sámi Parliament (Norway), seminar on the return of Sámi drums](https://sametinget.no/politikk/taler-og-innlegg/innlegg-pa-seminaret-ruoktot-tilbakeforing-av-samiske-trommer.49094.aspx) (community/government)
  - [RiddoDuottarMuseat: *Ruoktot* exhibition](https://rdm.no/en/de_samiske_samlinger-2/ruoktot-50ars-jubileumsutstilling-til-de-samiske-samlinger) (Sámi museum)
  - [Library of Congress: return of a Sámi drum](https://blogs.loc.gov/law/2022/02/return-of-a-smi-drum/) (government/legal)
- **Sharing:** **restricted.** The drums are sacred objects whose return is a live rights issue, and
  their painted figures are specific cosmologies. Personal joiks are owned. I found no Sámi guidance
  for game use. The Sámi Council has guidelines on ethical use of Sámi culture, which I did not
  retrieve; check them first.
- **Imagery check:** **restricted:** no drum-skin figures, no painted drums, no noaidi. Do not use
  gákti colours and patterns either; they mark family and region.
- **Game notes:** do not use.

#### Conifer and boreal shortlist (open)
1. **Väinämöinen's birch kantele: the weeping birch that becomes an instrument.** Open, has a primary
   text and a community cultural society, is literally a tree made into music that the forest gathers
   to hear, and is closest to the game's premise.
2. **The fossegrim: the fiddler under the waterfall.** Open, about a mossy forest stream, a teacher
   of music, and a natural fit for streams.
3. Runner-up: **tengu-bayashi**, music from an empty cedar mountain (open, about a landscape; use the
   sound, not the shrine imagery). Tapio and Mielikki's horn-answering grove is also open but thinner as a music figure.


### Rejected or not used

- **Jo-ga-oh / Dark Dance (Haudenosaunee):** a medicine society's ceremony; restricted.
- **The Big Drum and Midewiwin drum (Anishinaabe):** sacred, with origin stories that vary by
  community and protocols set by drum keepers ([Benton-Banai, PBS Wisconsin](https://pbswisconsin.org/video-stub-for-ojibwe-music)); restricted.
- **Dzunuk'wa and the Echo mask (Kwakwaka'wakw):** inherited privileges; restricted.
- **Haida and Tlingit crest figures (Raven, Thunderbird and others in crest form):** clan-owned crests;
  restricted. I found no music-specific open figure.
- **Coast Salish songs:** songs are personal and hereditary property ([Tulalip Lushootseed](https://tulaliplushootseed.com/songs/));
  restricted. I found no open forest music figure.
- **Sámi drum and joik:** sacred objects under repatriation; personal joiks are owned; restricted.
- **Evenki and Khanty shamanic drums:** ceremonial, with no community source on sharing found; not researched further.
- **Pan:** Mediterranean scrub and mountain, not this biome (better for another biome).
- **The Green Man:** a modern name (1939) for church carvings; no tie to music.
- **The Lorelei:** a river cliff and a literary creation (Brentano, 1801), not forest folklore.
- **The Pied Piper:** a town legend, not a landscape spirit.
- **Studio Ghibli's kodama look:** copyrighted design; use Sekien's or a fresh look instead.

- Cherokee: ask the Museum of the Cherokee People or EBCI before using even unnamed mountain drumming.

## Part 3: Grassland and savanna, and wetland


Research notes, 2026-11-18. Figures are grouped by landscape, not by culture. Each one is
marked **open**, **restricted** or **unclear** under the project's sharing rules.
"Game notes" are ideas only and are kept apart from the sourced material. Where sources
disagree, I say so. Nothing here is invented: if a claim had no source, I left it out.

Source kinds: **community** (the people's own institutions, or tellers and scholars from
the community), **intergovernmental** (UNESCO ICH, whose listings are nominated by the
states and communities concerned), **academic**, **museum**, **encyclopaedia**,
**popular** (travel sites, blogs, aggregators; used only to back up a point).


### 1. Grassland and savanna

**Cultures examined:** Eurasian steppe (Mongol, Kazakh, Tuvan, the Oghuz/Turkic Korkut
tradition), the Mande savanna of the Sahel and Sudan (Guinea and Mali; Sunjata/Sosso), the
Wodaabe/Fulani Sahel, Maasai, the Hausa (Bori), the Great Plains (Lakota, Dakota) and the
Australian tropical savanna (Arnhem Land, Yolŋu). I found no music figure tied to a
landscape for the Pampas.

#### 1.1 Korkyt Ata, maker of the first kobyz: Kazakh and wider Turkic, Syr Darya steppe
- **Tied to:** the kobyz (a horsehair bowed fiddle); imitating the sounds of nature (a wolf's
  howl); his camel; the open steppe; keeping death away.
- **Summary:** Korkyt is a legendary sage and bard. In the Kazakh telling he roams the
  world to escape death, gives up, and makes the first kyl-kobyz from the hide and hair of his
  camel Zhelmaya. He plays it without stopping, and death cannot take him while the
  music lasts. Across the Turkic world the same figure (Dede Korkut) is the elder bard of the
  Oghuz epic cycle. Tellings and dates differ.
- **Sources:**
  - [UNESCO ICH: Heritage of Dede Qorqud/Korkyt Ata/Dede Korkut, epic culture, folk tales and music (2018)](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/heritage-of-dede-qorqud-korkut-ata-dede-korkut-epic-culture-folk-tales-and-music-01399) (intergovernmental; jointly nominated by Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan and Turkey; it names the kobyz and its imitations of natural sounds)
  - [Times of Central Asia: Korkyt Ata's immortality journey](https://timesca.com/immortality-journey-korkyt-ata/) (popular/regional press)
- **Sharing check: OPEN.** The tradition is published epic and folklore, and the communities
  put it forward for UNESCO listing. Kazakhstan celebrates it publicly with a memorial complex
  and festivals. Its shamanic (baqsy) background is historical and is not a living ceremony
  someone holds closed.
- **Imagery check:** Open motifs: a bowed two-string fiddle with a hollow bowl and horsehair
  strings, a camel, steppe wind. The Kyzylorda memorial's steles are shaped like a kobyz and
  its pipes sound in the wind; it is a modern public monument, so take inspiration but do not
  copy it. Avoid copying particular baqsy costume or amulets.
- **Game notes (ideas):** a weathered figure carved into a standing stone, holding a
  bowl-fiddle. Waking it could bring the wind across the plain into tune, and its voice could
  keep something from fading for as long as it plays.

#### 1.2 The winged horse that became the horse-head fiddle: Mongol, steppe
- **Tied to:** the morin khuur (horse-head fiddle); horses; open steppe; music that tames
  animals.
- **Summary:** A herder is given a magical winged horse and rides it at night to his beloved
  far away. The horse dies, in most tellings through a jealous rival who cuts its wings. In
  his grief the herder makes the first fiddle from its body: bone for the neck, hide for the
  sound box, hair for the strings, the scroll carved as its head. Sources disagree on the
  hero's name (Namjil "the Cuckoo", or Sükhe/Suho) and on the details. UNESCO notes that the
  repertoire keeps tunes (tatlaga) meant to tame animals.
- **Sources:**
  - [UNESCO ICH: Traditional music of the Morin Khuur (proclaimed 2003, inscribed 2008)](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/00068) (intergovernmental; nominated by Mongolia)
  - [Elvik Dordzhiev, "About the morn khur", Cambridge repository](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/540f2a76-9ce2-4a75-8c21-3ed8d3eaea22) (academic archive of a recorded Kalmyk speaker; a community voice)
  - [Wikipedia: Morin khuur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morin_khuur) (encyclopaedia; for the name variants)
- **Sharing check: OPEN.** A national folk legend, retold freely in Mongolia and
  Inner Mongolia and through a state-nominated UNESCO listing.
- **Imagery check:** Open: a horse-head scroll, horsehair, a trapezoid sound box, wings, the
  steppe at night. The morin khuur is a national symbol, so treat it respectfully and do not
  make it comic. The winged horse is the folk legend's own image, not a clan mark.
- **Game notes (ideas):** a horse with folded wings carved into a lone tree. Singing to it
  could unfold the wings, and herd animals on the plain could answer its call.

#### 1.3 The lame kulan and the bard who could not speak the news: Kazakh, steppe
- **Tied to:** the dombra; a wild ass (kulan) of the steppe; a kuy (instrumental piece)
  that tells a story without words.
- **Summary:** Jochi, Genghis Khan's son, is killed on a kulan hunt; one version says a lame
  kulan turned on him. Anyone who brought the news faced death, so the bard Ketbuga plays the
  kuy "Aksak kulan" on the dombra and the khan understands. Since no man had spoken, the khan
  punishes the instrument: molten lead is poured into the dombra's sound hole. Versions differ
  on the details. Folk explanations of the dombra's hole and its mournful voice link back to
  this story.
- **Sources:**
  - [Qazaq Culture: Aksak kulan](https://qazaqculture.com/en/cultural-objects/ba3a34bb-0005-4ca3-9039-869481343c94) (community: a Kazakh cultural heritage portal)
  - [Kazinform: Ketbuga zhyrau](https://www.inform.kz/en/ketbuga-zhyrau_a2189546) (Kazakh national press agency)
  - [Bulletin of L.N. Gumilyov ENU, Philology: Legends of "Aksak kulan – Zhoshy khan"](https://bulphil.enu.kz/index.php/main/article/view/750) (academic, Kazakh university)
- **Sharing check: OPEN.** A secular historical legend and a concert piece that Kazakh
  musicians perform publicly. A 1968 Kazakh animated film retells it.
- **Imagery check:** Open: a two-string long-necked lute, a wild ass, the steppe. Do not
  depict Genghis Khan or Jochi; the game uses only unnamed spirits.
- **Game notes (ideas):** a limping wild ass carved in rock, with a sealed sound hole that
  song reopens. Its first sound is wordless, a theme that fits a game where the rack is never
  explained in text.

#### 1.4 Spirit-masters of place who answer in sound: Tuvan, steppe and mountain grassland
- **Tied to:** throat singing (khöömei), echo, wind, water; the idea that a landscape's
  spirit is heard in the sounds it makes.
- **Summary:** In Tuvan pastoral music, which rests on animism, mountains and rivers have
  spirits, and those spirits show themselves through the sounds the land makes as well as its
  shape. Echo off a cliff, a voice mixing with a brook, or wind may carry spiritual power, and
  singers make sound in response to the landscape. This is a general relationship to the land,
  not a single named deity.
- **Sources:**
  - [Levin & Süzükei, *Where Rivers and Mountains Sing* (Indiana UP, 2006)](https://www.perlego.com/book/973095/where-rivers-and-mountains-sing-sound-music-and-nomadism-in-tuva-and-beyond-pdf) (academic; co-written with Valentina Süzükei, a Tuvan musicologist, so in part a community source)
  - [Levin & Edgerton, "The Throat Singers of Tuva", *Scientific American* 1999](https://gauss.dartmouth.edu/~m5f10/Levin1999SciAm_tuvan_throat_singing.pdf) (academic/popular science)
  - [landscapemusic.org: Sketches of Nature](https://landscapemusic.org/essays/sketches-of-nature-landscape-music-in-the-central-asian-steppe/) (popular essay quoting Levin)
- **Sharing check: OPEN** for the general idea, which Tuvan musicians themselves explain
  to audiences. **Restricted, or at least unclear,** for anything from living shamanic
  practice: specific spirit-master (cher eezi) offerings, shaman rituals and their objects.
- **Imagery check:** Open: the singer outdoors, wind on grass, echo, the doshpuluur and
  igil. Do not copy a shaman's costume, drum painting or ovaa (cairn) offering practice.
- **Game notes (ideas):** a spirit of place with no fixed form, which answers the player's
  tone with an echo whose overtone lights up. This could be the grassland's echo grotto.

#### 1.5 The jinn's balafon (Sosso-Bala): Mande, West African savanna
- **Tied to:** the balafon (a wooden xylophone with gourd resonators); jinn; griot
  guardianship.
- **Summary:** Tradition says the first great balafon belonged to Sumaoro Kanté, the
  13th-century king of Sosso, who in some tellings received it from a jinn; others say he
  found it while hunting. The griot Bala Fasséké Kouyaté secretly played it, and after
  Sunjata's victory he became its guardian. The instrument is still kept at Niagassola,
  Guinea. Accounts of how the king got it differ.
- **Sources:**
  - [UNESCO ICH: Cultural space of Sosso-Bala (proclaimed 2001, inscribed 2008)](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/cultural-space-of-sosso-bala-00009) (intergovernmental; nominated by Guinea)
  - [Richmond Folk Festival: Malian balafon player keeps his family's tradition](https://www.richmondfolkfestival.org/rff-news/2019/9/22/malian-balafon-player-keeps-his-familys-musical-tradition-alive) (popular, but quotes a Kouyaté griot: a community voice)
- **Sharing check: the story is OPEN; the object is RESTRICTED.** The epic is public
  griot repertoire, performed and published (D. T. Niane's *Sundiata* comes from the griot
  Mamadou Kouyaté). The Sosso-Bala itself is a guarded object: only its guardian (the
  balatigui) plays it, and only on set occasions. So depict a balafon of the jinn kind, never
  that particular instrument or the Niagassola shrine.
- **Imagery check:** Open: wooden keys, rows of gourds, savanna, a jinn as an unseen
  helper. Restricted: the Niagassola hut and the actual Sosso-Bala, and copying griot
  families' identity, names or praise songs (jali lineages own those).
- **Game notes (ideas):** a xylophone half-grown into a baobab-like tree, its gourds still
  hanging. A hidden helper plays it at dusk.

#### 1.6 The elk, the woodpecker and the wind: the first courting flute, Lakota and Dakota, northern Plains
- **Tied to:** the cedar courting flute (siyotanka); a red-headed woodpecker; wind through a
  hollow branch; the elk.
- **Summary:** A young hunter tracking elk is lost overnight and hears a mournful sound. A
  woodpecker leads him to a cedar branch it has pecked hollow, and the wind blowing through it
  makes the music. He cannot play it until the woodpecker teaches him, in a vision after he
  prays alone on a hill. The flute wins him the woman he loves. Tellings vary, and the elk's
  courting call is linked to the flute's power.
- **Sources:**
  - [Wisdom of the Elders: Mary Louise Defender Wilson, Turtle Island Storytellers](https://wisdomoftheelders.org/program-202-turtle-island-storytellers/) (community: a Dakota elder telling it publicly on a Native-run program)
  - [LibreTexts: The Legend of the Flute (Brulé Sioux)](https://socialsci.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Anthropology/Cultural_Anthropology/Native_Peoples_of_North_America_(Stebbins)/06%3A_Is_There_a_Word_for_Art/6.01%3A_The_Legend_of_the_Flute_(Brule_Sioux)) (academic textbook reprinting Henry Crow Dog's 1967 telling, recorded by R. Erdoes)
  - [Akta Lakota Museum: Legend of the Flute](https://aktalakota.stjo.org/lakota-legends/legend-of-the-flute/) (museum and cultural centre run by St. Joseph's Indian School, a non-Native institution that serves Lakota students)
- **Sharing check: OPEN, with care.** Named Lakota and Dakota storytellers tell it publicly,
  and it is told as a story, not as a ceremony. Distinct from it and **restricted**: the
  flute's place in living courtship, flute-maker lineages, and anything in Lakota sacred
  ceremony (pipe, Sun Dance, White Buffalo Calf Woman; see the rejected list). Ideally ask a
  Lakota or Dakota cultural office (for example Sinte Gleska University or Oglala Lakota
  College) before using it.
- **Imagery check:** Open: a woodpecker, a hollow cedar branch, wind, an elk on open
  prairie. Avoid: elk-head flute effigies copied from museum pieces (artists' and family
  designs), Plains war bonnets and other regalia, painted designs from specific
  families or societies.
- **Game notes (ideas):** a woodpecker carved into a lone cedar on the plain. The holes it
  drills sound in the wind once the tree is sung awake. This is the strongest
  "wind across a plain" figure found, but use it last, and only after a community check.

#### Grassland shortlist (strongest open candidates, ranked)
1. **The winged horse and the horse-head fiddle (Mongol).** Clearly open, a national folk
   legend, a strong creature image, and steppe through and through.
2. **Korkyt Ata and the first kobyz (Kazakh/Turkic).** Open, UNESCO-listed by the communities
   themselves; the kobyz imitates nature's sounds, which suits a sound-in-the-landscape game.
3. Reserve: **the lame kulan (Kazakh)**, an open steppe creature with wordless music.
   **Tuvan spirit-masters of place** fit the brief best as an idea but are a relationship, not
   a figure; use only the open general concept.


### 2. Wetland

**Cultures examined:** Finnish and Karelian (Kalevala), Swedish and Norwegian (Näcken,
Fossegrim), East Slavic (rusalki), Irish (the Children of Lir), ancient Greek (Syrinx at the
river Ladon), Persian Sufi poetry (Rumi's reed), Japanese (Benzaiten), West and Central
African water spirits (Mami Wata), the Upper Rio Negro and Amazon (Jurupari flutes; the boto),
the Sumerians and Marsh Arabs, and the Inner Niger Delta (Faro). For the Okavango and the Sudd
I found no sourced music figure tied to a landscape.

#### 2.1 The pike-jaw kantele: Finnish/Karelian, lakes
- **Tied to:** the kantele (a plucked zither); a giant pike's jawbone; lakes; a second
  kantele of weeping birch; all nature gathering to listen.
- **Summary:** In the Kalevala (poems 40–41) Väinämöinen kills a monstrous pike, makes the
  first kantele from its jawbone and plays it, and every creature of forest, air and water
  comes to listen until they all weep. After it is lost in the sea (poems 42–43) he makes a new
  one from a weeping birch (poem 44). At the end he leaves his kantele and songs to his people.
- **Sources:**
  - [Kalevala Society: What happens in the Kalevala](https://kalevalaseura.fi/en/what-happens-in-the-kalevala/) (community: the Finnish Kalevala Society)
  - [Public Domain Review: the Kalevala](https://publicdomainreview.org/collection/kalevala) (encyclopaedic/popular, public-domain text)
- **Sharing check: OPEN.** Folklore collected and published in 1835/1849, a national epic,
  public domain, and promoted by the Kalevala Society. The Karelian runo-singing tradition is
  living but shares its songs openly.
- **Imagery check:** Open: a pike, a birch, a lake, a box zither with five strings, Finnish
  ornament in a general sense. Avoid copying Akseli Gallen-Kallela's paintings, which are
  famous artworks (public domain, but they would become this figure's look).
- **Game notes (ideas):** a huge pike's jaw carved into a lakeside rock. Singing to it could
  string it into a kantele, and animals could gather at the shore to listen. A later birch
  version could tie into the scrub oaks.

#### 2.2 Näcken and the Fossegrim: the fiddler in the water, Swedish and Norwegian, streams, millraces, lakes
- **Tied to:** the fiddle; running water, lakes, waterfalls; teaching music for a price.
- **Summary:** Näcken is a water spirit who plays the fiddle so beautifully that listeners
  are drawn to the water, sometimes to drown. In Norway the Fossegrim of waterfalls
  will teach a fiddler to play if paid with an offering such as meat. Some tellings make the
  music so strong that trees and waterfalls dance. Both figures are dangerous as well as
  beautiful.
- **Sources:**
  - [Wikipedia: Fossegrim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossegrim) (encyclopaedia, with references to Norwegian folklore collections)
  - [Wikipedia: Nixie / Näck](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Nokk) (encyclopaedia)
  - Community check to do: Nordiska museet and the Norwegian Folk Music Archive did not come up
    in my search. Add a primary source (Hyltén-Cavallius; Swedish folk tale archives) before
    using this.
- **Sharing check: OPEN.** Historical Nordic folk belief, published since the 19th
  century (Ernst Josephson's painting *Näcken*, Swedish folk tale collections). Nobody
  holds it closed.
- **Imagery check:** Open: a figure half in the water with a fiddle, a waterfall, a millrace,
  the Hardanger fiddle in a general sense. Avoid copying Josephson's or Theodor Kittelsen's
  compositions. Avoid the 19th-century naked man trope if it clashes with the tone.
- **Game notes (ideas):** a fiddler carved into a boulder where a stream drops. Woken, it
  teaches: perhaps it unlocks a bowed module recipe, paid for with an offering, in keeping
  with "recipes made at a tempo".

#### 2.3 Syrinx, the reed bed that became the pipes: ancient Greek, the river Ladon in Arcadia
- **Tied to:** river reeds; panpipes; wind through cut reeds.
- **Summary:** Syrinx, a naiad of the river Ladon, flees Pan and begs her water-sisters to
  change her. She becomes a stand of marsh reeds. Pan hears his sigh make a sound through them,
  cuts reeds of unequal length, joins them with wax and plays the first panpipes, which keep
  her name (Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 1.689–712).
- **Sources:**
  - [Theoi: Syrinx](https://theoi.com/Nymphe/NympheSyrinx.html) (encyclopaedia of classical texts, quoting the primary sources)
  - [Theoi: the Ladonides, quoting Ovid](https://theoi.com/Nymphe/NymphaiLadonides.html) (as above)
- **Sharing check: OPEN.** An ancient religion known from published literature. A small
  contemporary Hellenic polytheist community exists; the myth is not held closed.
- **Imagery check:** Open: reeds of graded length bound with wax, a river, a reed nymph.
  The story is a pursuit and an escape from assault, so show the reeds and the pipes, not the
  chase.
- **Game notes (ideas):** a reed bed that whistles in the wind once woken, where the player
  can cut tuned reeds. This fits naturally with the game's reeds (6 to a stream segment). It
  should be a spirit of the reed bed only, with no Pan figure.

#### 2.4 The swans who sang on the lake: Irish, Lough Derravaragh, County Westmeath
- **Tied to:** song; swans; a specific lake; listeners gathering on the shore.
- **Summary:** In *Oidheadh Chlainne Lir*, the four children of Lir are turned into swans by
  their stepmother and must spend 300 years on Lough Derravaragh, then 300 on the Sea of Moyle,
  then 300 on Erris. She lets them keep their voices and their song, which surpasses all
  earthly music. While they are on the lake, people come and camp on its shore to listen.
- **Sources:**
  - [Ask About Ireland: The Children of Lir, Lough Derravaragh](https://askaboutireland.ie/reading-room/environment-geography/physical-landscape/lakelands-of-westmeath/lough-derravaragh/the-children-of-lir) (community: Irish public libraries' heritage portal)
  - [Libraries Ireland: The Children of Lir (PDF)](https://www.librariesireland.ie/sites/default/files/media/file-uploads/2018-10/The-Children-of-Lir-Senior.pdf) (community: Irish public libraries)
  - [Wikipedia: Children of Lir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children_of_Lir) (encyclopaedia)
- **Sharing check: OPEN.** A medieval Irish literary tale, taught in Irish schools and
  retold by Irish public institutions.
- **Imagery check:** Open: four swans, a lake, mist, a song. The tale has a well-known
  public monument (the Garden of Remembrance sculpture in Dublin); do not copy it.
- **Game notes (ideas):** four swans carved into a lakeside stone. Once sung to, the swans
  appear on the refilled lake and sing a four-part chord. A natural goal for "a pond that
  refills".

#### 2.5 Benzaiten, the lute goddess of flowing water: Japanese, Lake Biwa and temple ponds
- **Tied to:** the biwa (lute); water and "everything that flows"; lakes, islands and
  ponds; a dragon or snake as her attendant.
- **Summary:** Benzaiten (from the Hindu river goddess Sarasvatī) is a goddess of music,
  speech and flowing water, usually shown playing the biwa. Her shrines stand on islands and
  by ponds. One of the three great ones is on Chikubushima, an island in Lake Biwa, said to be
  her home since a monk was sent there in 724. She is one of the Seven Gods of Good Fortune.
- **Sources:**
  - [Hōgon-ji, Northern Lake Biwa tourism (Nagahama)](https://kitabiwako.jp/en/travel_guide/spot/detail?id=1815) (community: local official tourism body)
  - [Wikipedia: Hōgon-ji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C5%8Dgon-ji) (encyclopaedia)
  - [Wikiwand: Benzaiten](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Benten) (encyclopaedia)
- **Sharing check: OPEN, as a living public religion, with care.** She is worshipped today
  in public temples that welcome visitors and sell her image; nothing about her is closed.
  But she is a living deity, so treat her with respect (no comic use, no defacing, no
  "corrupting"). Some temple images are hibutsu (hidden Buddhas shown only on set occasions):
  do not depict those particular images.
- **Imagery check:** Open: a woman with a lute by water, a white snake or dragon, a lotus,
  an island torii. Avoid copying a specific temple statue, especially a hidden one.
  Because she is a recognisable living deity, an unnamed "lute spirit of the lake" is the
  safer way to draw on her.
- **Game notes (ideas):** a lute-player carved on an island stone in a lake. Waking her
  could let the water run clear, which matches "music is a flowing thing". A dragon form could
  coil through the reeds.

#### 2.6 Rusalki: the singing water maidens, East Slavic (Ukrainian, Russian, Belarusian), rivers and lakes
- **Tied to:** song and round dances (khorovod) on the banks; Rusalia week in early summer;
  moisture for the fields.
- **Summary:** Rusalki are water spirits who leave the rivers in early summer. Until St
  Peter's day they play on the banks all night, swing in the branches, dance and sing rusalka
  songs. Older belief links them to the water that makes the fields grow; 19th-century
  accounts make them dangerous, luring young men with song. Villagers marked Rusalia week by
  dressing girls as rusalki, weaving wreaths and singing rusalka songs.
- **Sources:**
  - [Encyclopedia of Ukraine: Rusalka](https://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/pages%5CR%5CU%5CRusalkaIT.htm) (community: Canadian Institute of Ukrainian Studies)
  - [Encyclopedia of Ukraine: Rusalka songs](https://www.encyclopediaofukraine.com/pages%5CR%5CU%5CRusalkasongsIT.htm) (community)
  - [Wikipedia: Slavic water spirits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_water_spirits) (encyclopaedia)
- **Sharing check: OPEN.** Historical folk belief, documented by the communities' own
  scholars, with songs collected and published. Contemporary Rodnovery is small and does not
  hold this closed.
- **Imagery check:** Open: wreaths, willows, birches, the khorovod, long loose hair, moonlit
  banks. Be careful with Ukrainian embroidery (vyshyvanka) patterns, which carry regional and
  family meanings: inspired by, never copied.
- **Game notes (ideas):** spirits that rise from a refilled pond at dusk in a round dance. Their
  song waters the fields, which suits the older, life-giving reading.

#### 2.7 The reed torn from the reed bed: Persian, Rumi's *Masnavi*
- **Tied to:** the ney (reed flute); the reed bed; the lament of separation.
- **Summary:** The *Masnavi* opens with "Listen to the reed, how it tells a tale, complaining
  of separations." The ney was cut from its reed bed, and its whole song is longing to return.
  This is a poetic image, not a deity: the reed bed itself as the source of music.
- **Sources:**
  - [Dar al-Masnavi: The Song of the Reed, with translations side by side](https://dar-al-masnavi.org/reedsong.html) (academic/devotional; Ibrahim Gamard's translation project)
- **Sharing check: OPEN** for the poem, which is published, widely translated and
  recited in public. The Mevlevi sema ceremony (UNESCO-listed) is a living devotional practice;
  do not reproduce its ceremony, dress or turning.
- **Imagery check:** Open: a reed bed, a cut reed flute, wind. Avoid Mevlevi tall felt hats,
  white skirts and the whirling posture.
- **Game notes (ideas):** a theme rather than a figure: a cut reed that "longs" for its bed
  and sounds fullest when played beside living reeds. A possible design hook for stream reeds.

#### Wetland shortlist (strongest open candidates, ranked)
1. **The pike-jaw kantele (Finnish).** Fully open, it is lake and creature and instrument
   together, and its "every creature comes to listen" is the game's theme exactly.
2. **The swans who sang on the lake (Irish).** Open, tied to one real lake, it is about song,
   and it ends with people gathering at the shore to listen.
3. Reserve: **Syrinx's reed bed (Greek)** fits the game's reeds and streams best mechanically.
   **Näcken/Fossegrim** offers a "teaches you music" role, but needs a primary Nordic source
   added first.


### Rejected or set aside

- **White Buffalo Calf Woman (Lakota):** the bringer of the sacred pipe and the seven
  ceremonies. A living sacred figure, central to ceremony. Restricted.
- **Lakota and Dakota ceremonial songs and drum (Sun Dance, Inipi, Hunka):** living
  ceremony. Restricted.
- **Yolŋu yidaki (didjeridu) origin stories and other Arnhem Land and Top End savanna
  Dreaming songs:** knowledge is owned by clans and often limited by gender or initiation
  ([AIATSIS access and use policy](https://aiatsis.gov.au/sites/default/files/2020-09/aiatsis-access-and-use-policy-2018_0.pdf);
  [ATSILIRN Protocol 6](https://www.nsla.org.au/resources/cslp-collections/protocol6/)).
  Restricted unless a specific clan publishes a story for open use.
- **Hausa Bori spirits (Sahel):** a living possession cult with music at its centre. No
  community source found saying how it is shared. Unclear; left out.
- **Wodaabe Gerewol (Sahel):** a living courtship dance festival, not a mythic figure, and
  it would mean copying living people's looks. Left out.
- **Maasai Enkai (Engai):** a sky and rain deity with no sourced link to music. Out of
  scope.
- **Jurupari / Kowai sacred flutes (Upper Rio Negro: Baniwa, Tukano, Baré):** women and the
  uninitiated may not see the flutes, on pain of severe punishment
  ([Redalyc ethnography](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/3940/394069977010/394069977010.pdf);
  [Boletim Goeldi](https://www.scielo.br/j/bgoeldi/a/ZgvHMDn6TQXBGn3TDY5FMQG/?lang=pt)).
  Restricted, and a clear example of what the game must not use.
- **Mami Wata (West and Central Africa, diaspora):** a living religion with priests,
  initiates and shrines
  ([Smithsonian NMAfA, *Mami Wata* exhibition](https://dashboard.si.edu/es/newsdesk/releases/water-spirit-focus-national-museum-african-art-exhibition);
  [Drewal, *African Arts* 2008](https://staff.washington.edu/ellingsn/Drewal-Mami_Wata-AfAr.2008.41.2.pdf)).
  Restricted for practice and shrine imagery. Her link to music is not her main domain, and
  the famous snake-charmer chromolithograph is a specific image.
- **The boto (Amazon river dolphin who comes to dances):** open Brazilian river folklore,
  but the music link is thin (it attends a party; it does not make music) and its seduction
  plot does not fit. Set aside.
- **Faro (Bambara and Bozo, Inner Niger Delta):** the main published accounts come from
  disputed colonial-era ethnography (Griaule and Dieterlen), and I found no community source
  on music or sharing. Unclear; left out.
- **Sumerian reed imagery (Ea speaking through the reed wall; balag lament drum; Marsh Arab
  reed houses):** there is real reed and sound imagery, but no music figure tied to a marsh
  that I could source as one. Worth a second, dedicated search.
- **Okavango and Sudd (Dinka and Nuer song oxen):** song culture is rich, but I found no
  mythic music figure, and the song-ox tradition is living personal practice. Left out.
- **Tuvan spirit-master offerings and shaman drums:** living shamanic practice. Restricted
  (the open general idea is kept under 1.4).

## Part 4: Tropical forest and mountain


Researched 2026-11-18 by web search. Each source is marked with its kind. Where I read only
a summary or a secondary citation and not the primary text, the entry says so. Nothing here
is invented. If a detail could not be sourced, it is left out or marked unclear.

Sharing key: **open** means openly shared by its own community, or a published literary or
folkloric tradition. **restricted** means initiate-only, gender-restricted, or a sacred
object or ceremony. **unclear** means no community source says how it is shared.


### 1. Tropical forest

**Cultures looked at:** Mbuti and Aka (BaAka) of the Central African rainforest; Akan (Asante)
and Yoruba of the West African forest; Tukano, Baniwa and Baré of the northwest Amazon;
Tupi-derived Brazilian Amazon folklore; K'iche' Maya (Popol Vuh); Temiar of the Malaysian
rainforest; Kaluli (Bosavi) of Papua New Guinea.

#### 1.1 The muni bird: the boy who became a bird — Kaluli (Bosavi), Papua New Guinea
- **Tied to:** birdsong, weeping song, the rainforest canopy; a creature (the muni bird,
  Beautiful Fruit-Dove).
- **Summary:** A boy is refused food by his older sister. He turns into a muni bird, and
  his falling, descending call is said to be the lost boy's voice crying to her. For the
  Kaluli, birds are the voices of the departed. The muni's descending melody is the model
  for women's sung-weeping (*sa-yelab*) and for songs that move listeners to tears. Steven
  Feld built *Sound and Sentiment* around this myth.
- **Sources:**
  - [Feld, *Sound and Sentiment* (1982/1990/2012), ch. 1 "The Boy Who Became a Muni Bird"](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv113180h) (academic; I saw the table of contents only)
  - [St Andrews student paper citing Feld 2012 on the muni call and weeping](https://artsanddivinityfacultyjournal.wp.st-andrews.ac.uk/files/2023/05/MU1006-SUBMISSION_Natasha-Currie.pdf) (academic, secondary)
  - [BYUH review of the book](https://lir.byuh.edu/index.php/pacific/article/download/2201/2126) (academic review)
  - [Smithsonian Folkways, *Voices of the Rainforest*](https://folkways.si.edu/voices-of-the-rainforest/world/music/album/smithsonian) (museum)
  - [MacArthur Foundation on the Bosavi People's Fund](https://www.macfound.org/fellows/class-of-1991/steven-feld) (encyclopaedia/institutional)
- **Sharing check: open, with care.** The myth was told to Feld for publication over decades
  of collaboration. His recordings and the 2019 film (made with Jeremiah Rah Richards) return
  royalties to the Bosavi People's Fund, and Kaluli families have kept collaborating on
  publication. That is reasonable evidence of consent to share the story. Two cautions:
  - Birds as the voices of the dead touch real mourning practice.
  - The *gisalo* ceremonial song, with its dancer and burning, is a specific ceremony and
    should not be used.
  - I found no current statement from Bosavi people themselves; Feld's work is the channel.
- **Imagery:**
  - Safe: a generic canopy fruit-dove, green and grey with a pale throat, and a falling
    four-note call (the bird is a public species).
  - Avoid: Kaluli ceremonial dress (feathered *gisalo* or *ko:luba* costume, body paint) and
    any depiction of mourning.
- **Game ideas:** A canopy spirit of a dove whose falling call is the first tune heard in
  the forest. Restored by answering its descending phrase. Its "carving" could be the bird
  in a tree hollow.

#### 1.2 The forest that sleeps and is sung awake — Mbuti (Ituri) and Aka (BaAka), Central African rainforest
- **Tied to:** polyphonic song, the forest as a living, listening being; echo under the
  canopy.
- **Summary:** The Mbuti regard the forest as a good parent, and when misfortune comes they
  say the forest has fallen asleep. The *molimo* ceremony uses nightly singing, with a
  trumpet that answers it, to wake the forest. Aka communities likewise accompany camp,
  hunting and funeral rituals with improvised four-part polyphony that every member learns.
- **Sources:**
  - [Encyclopedia.com, "Efe and Mbuti"](https://www.encyclopedia.com/international/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/efe-and-mbuti) (encyclopaedia)
  - [HRAF summary of Turnbull's ethnographies](https://ehrafworldcultures.yale.edu/cultures/fo04/summary) (academic)
  - [UNESCO, Polyphonic singing of the Aka Pygmies of Central Africa](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/polyphonic-singing-of-the-aka-pygmies-of-central-africa-00082?RL=00082) (intergovernmental; the nomination was made with the state, not clearly by the community)
- **Sharing check: split.**
  - **The *molimo* trumpet and ceremony are restricted.** Women and children must stay in
    their huts with the doors closed, and the trumpet is hidden in the forest between
    uses. Never show or name it.
  - The general idea, a forest that listens and is woken by song, is published widely
    (Turnbull's *The Forest People*, 1961). It is **unclear** whether Mbuti people endorse
    that sharing, and Turnbull is an outsider author.
  - Aka polyphony as a practice is openly presented (UNESCO). Their masked forest spirits,
    such as Ejengi, are initiation-bound; I did not source them, so they are left out.
- **Imagery:**
  - Safe: interlocking voices, a dense canopy, leaf shelters.
  - Avoid: trumpets, masks, and anything resembling the molimo or forest-spirit masks.
  - Caution: images of "Pygmy" people are a long-standing exploitative trope; depict no
    people.
- **Game ideas:** A forest is not a figure but a state. The canopy itself "sleeps", and
  layered voices, four interlocking patterns, wake it. Best used as a mechanic, not as a
  carved figure.

#### 1.3 Hun Batz and Hun Chuen, the howler-monkey musicians — K'iche' Maya, Guatemala (Popol Vuh)
- **Tied to:** flute and song, the arts; monkeys of the forest canopy.
- **Summary:** In the Popol Vuh, the elder half-brothers of the Hero Twins are flautists,
  singers, scribes and artisans. They bully the Twins and are lured up a tree, where they
  become monkeys. When the Twins play the flute they come down to dance, and they are
  remembered as patrons of music and the arts. Classic Maya art shows howler-monkey gods as
  scribes and artists, and some Copán sculptures may show them as musicians.
- **Sources:**
  - [Wikipedia, Howler monkey gods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howler_monkey_gods) (encyclopaedia)
  - Popol Vuh, trans. Dennis Tedlock (1985/1996) (academic translation; not opened this session; check before use)
- **Sharing check: open.** The Popol Vuh was written down in the 16th century by K'iche'
  nobles themselves. It is widely published, and Guatemala treats it as national heritage.
  - Sensitivity: B'atz' (monkey) is a day of the living K'iche' calendar kept by day-keepers
    (*ajq'ij*). Do not use day-sign glyphs or calendar ritual.
  - The episode humiliates the brothers, so a pure-hero framing would invert the story;
    keep the patron-of-arts sense.
- **Imagery:**
  - Safe: a howler monkey with flute or brush, and the canopy.
  - Inspiration only: Classic Maya ceramic style, such as codex-style vases. Do not copy
    specific vessels or glyphs.
  - Avoid: calendar glyphs.
- **Game ideas:** Two monkey spirits carved into a tree, a pair whose duet needs two voices.
  The player's flute brings them down from the canopy.

#### 1.4 The spirit of the drum-tree — Akan (Asante), Ghana
- **Tied to:** talking drums (*atumpan*), the forest tree whose wood becomes the drum.
- **Summary:** The atumpan is carved from the wood of a forest tree, given as Tweneboa
  (*Cordia millenii*). Traditionally an offering was made and prayers said to the tree
  before it was felled. In performance, the master drummer's opening invocation addresses
  the spirits of every material of the drum: the tree, the bark used for the strings, the
  wood of the pegs, and the elephant-ear skin. On this view, the drum speaks with the voice
  of the tree.
- **Sources:**
  - [Royal Museum for Central Africa, atumpan page (citing Nketia 1957)](https://music.africamuseum.be/instruments/english/ghana/ashanti/atumpan.html) (museum)
  - [Ghana News Agency, "Talking drums in contemporary Ghana: the Atumpan in focus"](https://gna.org.gh/2023/03/talking-drums-in-contemporary-ghana-the-atumpan-in-focus/) (community/national press)
  - [Journal of African Music article (Nketia line)](https://journal.ru.ac.za/index.php/africanmusic/article/view/225/164) (academic)
  - J. H. Kwabena Nketia, "The Akan drummer" (academic, by a Ghanaian scholar; I saw it only via citation)
- **Sharing check: open for the general idea, unclear in detail.** Nketia was an Akan scholar
  who published this for teaching, and the Ghana News Agency discusses it openly.
  - The exact invocation texts belong to royal court drumming and the chieftaincy, a living
    institution, so do not reproduce them.
  - The tree species is not settled across sources.
- **Imagery:**
  - Safe: a tall forest tree, and a waisted drum shape in general.
  - Avoid: adinkra or stool symbols tied to particular royal houses, and the court's
    ceremonial regalia.
- **Game ideas:** A tree spirit whose trunk sounds a deep two-tone voice (atumpan drums come
  in male/female pairs). Fits "machines are instruments" and the rule that a tree's wood
  holds a voice.

#### 1.5 The uirapuru, the bird all others stop to hear — Brazilian Amazon (Tupi-derived folklore)
- **Tied to:** birdsong. The real musician wren (*Cyphorhinus arada*) sings in consonant
  intervals.
- **Summary:** In Amazonian folklore, when the uirapuru sings, every other bird falls silent
  to listen. Its song or body is said to bring love and luck. Heitor Villa-Lobos's ballet
  *Uirapuru* (1917/1934) retold it as a bird that turns into a youth. The bird itself is
  studied for singing octaves, fifths and fourths.
- **Sources:**
  - [Interlude, "The Legends and Sounds of Amazonia"](https://interlude.hk/the-legends-and-sounds-of-amazonia-heitor-villa-lobos-and-philip-glass/) (popular)
  - [JungleDragon, musician wren](https://www.jungledragon.com/specie/33967/musician-wren.html) (popular/natural history)
  - [eBird, musician wren](https://ebird.org/species/muswre2) (scientific)
- **Sharing check: open as Brazilian popular folklore.** The name is Tupi, but I found no
  Indigenous community source for the legend's original form, and the Villa-Lobos version
  is a composer's retelling.
  - One belief is to kill the bird and bury it at a shop door for luck. Leave that out.
- **Imagery:**
  - Safe: a small brown wren with bright song, and a hush that falls over the forest.
  - Avoid: Indigenous Amazonian body-paint designs or featherwork.
- **Game ideas:** A spirit whose song silences all ambient birds while it sings, an audio
  ducking moment. Its intervals (octave, fifth, fourth) could set what tunings it accepts.

#### 1.6 Dream-song spirit guides — Temiar, Malaysian rainforest
- **Tied to:** dream songs given by spirits of flowers, fruit trees, mountains and rivers;
  healing.
- **Summary:** Temiar mediums receive songs in dreams from spirit guides who live in the
  rainforest's plants and landscape. In one account the scent of a flowering plant became a
  spirit who gave a song. The songs are then sung in community ceremonies for healing.
- **Sources:**
  - [Marina Roseman, *Healing Sounds from the Malaysian Rainforest* (UC Press, 1993)](https://www.ucpress.edu/books/healing-sounds-from-the-malaysian-rainforest/paper) (academic)
  - [Smithsonian Folkways, *Dream Songs and Healing Sounds in the Rainforests of Malaysia*](https://folkways.si.edu/dream-songs-and-healing-sounds-in-the-rainforests-of-malaysia/islamica-psychology-health-world/album/smithsonian) (museum)
- **Sharing check: unclear leaning restricted.** Individual songs belong to the medium who
  received them and are used in healing ceremonies. I found no Temiar community source on
  how these are shared. The flower-song example came via a secondary blog citing Roseman
  pp. 52–58, not the book itself.
- **Imagery:** Avoid Temiar ceremonial leaf ornaments and the healing-ceremony setting.
  Generic flowering plants are fine.
- **Game ideas:** Only the general idea that a plant's bloom can "give" a melody, which
  matches the game's foliage mechanic. No specific figure.

#### Tropical forest shortlist (open)
1. **Muni bird, the falling canopy call (Kaluli).** Strongest: a creature, a landscape, a
   melody and emotion, with a consenting publication history. Avoid ceremony and mourning
   imagery.
2. **Howler-monkey musicians (K'iche' Maya, Popol Vuh).** A text written by the community's
   own authors, a flute, and the canopy. Avoid calendar glyphs.
- Reserve: the drum-tree spirit (Akan), as a general idea only, not the invocations.

#### Tropical forest rejected
- **Molimo trumpet (Mbuti):** restricted; women and children may not see it.
- **Jurupari / Kowai sacred flutes (Tukano, Baniwa, Baré):** restricted; women and
  non-initiates are forbidden to see them, with severe sanctions
  ([Baniwa ethnography](https://core.ac.uk/download/223854015.pdf),
  [SciELO, Upper Rio Negro](https://www.scielo.br/j/bgoeldi/a/ZgvHMDn6TQXBGn3TDY5FMQG/?lang=pt)).
- **Ejengi and other Aka masked spirits:** initiation-bound and not sourced.
- **Àyàn Agalú, orisha of the drum (Yoruba):** a living religion with drumming-lineage
  worship and annual sacrifice
  ([Seattle Art Museum](https://www1.seattleartmuseum.org/Exhibit/Archive/longsteps/agalu.htm));
  practice also continues in Cuban Aña. Do not render a living deity as a carved
  "dormant" figure.
- **Curupira (Brazil):** a forest guardian whose whistle misleads; a trickster and threat,
  not a music figure, and the whistle details are only weakly sourced.
- **Balinese and Javanese gamelan spirits:** a living Hindu and Javanese practice (offerings
  to *pusaka* gamelan); not researched far enough to source.


### 2. Mountain

**Cultures looked at:** Quechua and Aymara of the Bolivian highlands (Northern Potosí);
Swiss Alpine herders; Japanese mountain folklore; Greek mountain myth; the Himalaya (Hindu
and Buddhist kinnara); the Ethiopian highlands (Ethiopian Orthodox); the Caucasus and the
Rocky Mountains (looked at briefly, nothing usable sourced).

#### 2.1 Yamabiko, the mountain echo — Japan
- **Tied to:** echo in valleys and canyons; a mountain spirit, often heard rather than
  seen.
- **Summary:** Yamabiko is both the word for a mountain echo and the spirit said to cause
  it, answering your voice from across a valley. Toriyama Sekien's *Gazu Hyakki Yagyō*
  (1776) draws it as a small dog-monkey-like creature. Local variants include:
  - a "calling bird" in Tottori;
  - an eerie voice from deep in the mountains in Kōchi;
  - a yamabiko rock in Nagano that returns people's words.
  When the echo is thought to come from a tree spirit, it is called *kodama*.
- **Sources:**
  - [Wikipedia, Yamabiko (folklore)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamabiko_(folklore)) (encyclopaedia; cites Foster's *Book of Yōkai*, Komatsu, Yanagita)
  - [yokai.jp, Yamabiko Rock at Koiwatake](https://yokai.jp/en/place/koiwatake-yamabiko-iwa) (popular/community, Japanese)
  - [BYU Bakemono collection](https://bakemono.lib.byu.edu/?p=198) (museum/library)
- **Sharing check: open.** It is Edo-period published folklore, Sekien's prints are public
  domain, and Japanese yōkai culture openly shares and remakes it.
  - Sensitivity: in some places mountain *kami* are worshipped at living Shinto shrines.
    Keep it a folklore yōkai and use no shrine imagery (torii, shimenawa).
- **Imagery:**
  - Safe: a small furry creature on a crag, or a rock that answers.
  - Free to use but not to trace: Sekien's own designs are public domain; make the game's
    figure its own.
  - Avoid: Studio Ghibli's kodama look, which is copyrighted and widely recognised.
- **Game ideas:** A cliff spirit that returns the player's phrase late, a delay or echo
  module found in rock. The answering rock is the carving.

#### 2.2 Echo, the mountain nymph — Greek (Mount Cithaeron)
- **Tied to:** echo, song, mountainsides; an oread, a mountain nymph.
- **Summary:**
  - In Ovid, Hera curses Echo so that she can only repeat the last words of others.
  - In Longus's *Daphnis and Chloe*, she is a gifted singer who refuses Pan. He maddens the
    herdsmen, who tear her apart. Earth hides the pieces, and her song remains, imitating
    every sound.
  - Other versions (Nonnus, Apuleius) are gentler.
- **Sources:**
  - [Wikipedia, Echo (mythology)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echo_(mythology)) (encyclopaedia)
  - [Britannica, Echo](https://www.britannica.com/print/article/177977) (encyclopaedia)
  - Longus, *Daphnis and Chloe* 3.23; Ovid, *Metamorphoses* 3 (classical texts, public domain)
- **Sharing check: open.** Classical published literature with no living cult community.
  The Longus version is violent, so use the "song scattered into the earth" image, not the
  killing.
- **Imagery:** Safe: Greek vase-painting or relief style as inspiration; this is public
  domain heritage. A figure dissolving into stone.
- **Game ideas:** Echo's song "scattered through the earth" fits a figure found carved in
  rock across many places. Fragments that each answer one note could be reassembled.
  Pan overlaps with the scrubland researcher.

#### 2.3 Sirinu (sirena), spirits of springs and waterfalls who tune instruments — Quechua and Aymara, Northern Potosí, Bolivia
- **Tied to:** springs, waterfalls, rocks; tuning instruments (*pinkillu* flutes,
  charango); new melodies each year; the rainy and Carnival season.
- **Summary:**
  - In the Bolivian highlands, sirinus are ambiguous water beings said to be the source of
    music. They come out of the earth at night in the Carnival season.
  - Musicians leave new instruments overnight at springs, waterfalls and rocks so the
    sirinu will tune them.
  - New melodies (*wayñus*) are received by rivers, often in sleep, with an offering. As
    one musician puts it, they "cannot be made at home."
  - Sirinus are also blamed for excess, abduction and danger.
  - Pinkillu music is played only from Todos Santos (November) to Carnival.
- **Sources:**
  - [Correo del Sur, "Chuturkurikasqani (Trance): El encantamiento de las sirinus" (Alina Cuentas Cedro, 2020, drawing on Stobart, Gérard, Harris)](https://correodelsur.com/ecos/20200301/chuturkurikasqani-trance-el-encantamiento-de-las-sirinus.html) (Bolivian regional press; national-community)
  - [Stobart, "Beyond Pulsating Dissonance" (2023)](https://pure.royalholloway.ac.uk/files/56130162/Stobart_2023_Beyond_Pulsating_Dissonance_-_prepublication_version.pdf) (academic; not fully read)
  - Henry Stobart, *Music and the Poetics of Production in the Bolivian Andes* (Ashgate, 2006) (academic; not opened)
  - [Journal of Music Archaeology, list of terms including sirinu/sirena](https://jma.vlg.oeaw.ac.at/index.php/jma/article/download/4/7/824) (academic)
- **Sharing check: unclear, possibly restricted.** This is a living practice. The specific
  places where musicians take instruments are described as secret. Olivia Harris reports a
  warning that recording pinkillu music could bring dangerous Carnival devils into the
  living world.
  - The general belief, that water spirits tune instruments and give tunes, is discussed
    openly in the Bolivian press and in scholarship.
  - I found no statement from a Northern Potosí community on outside use.
  - Treat it as **unclear**: use only the general idea, never a named site, ritual or
    recording.
- **Imagery:**
  - Safe: a spring or waterfall among bare rock, and an instrument left on a stone.
  - Avoid: the mermaid-with-charango figures carved in colonial highland churches (mentioned
    via Turino; a church setting), Carnival devil costumes, and the *awayu* textile designs
    of specific communities.
- **Game ideas:** Leaving a module at a mountain spring overnight and finding it tuned in
  the morning fits the game's quantised-knob mechanic. Seasonal: active only in the wet
  season. Needs a check with Andean community advisers before shipping.

#### 2.4 The spirits who gave the alphorn — Swiss Alps (Haslital, Bernese Oberland)
- **Tied to:** alphorn, echo, high summer pasture, cowbells.
- **Summary:** One summer night on the Bahlisalp above the Haslital, the young herdsman Res
  wakes to find three strangers in the dairy hut: a giant at the cheese cauldron, a pale,
  golden-haired herdsman, and a green-clad huntsman. The pale one plays a strange wooden
  staff whose notes wake the cattle and their bells. The giant offers three bowls of whey:
  red for strength, green for the best pastures, white for the horn. Res chooses white. At
  dawn he finds an alphorn by the hearth. When he blows it, the mountains echo back like
  spirit voices, and the valley wonders whether the mountains have begun to sing.
- **Sources:**
  - [swissinfo.ch, "Legends & Myths"](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/legends---myths/7687150) (national public broadcaster; community/popular)
  - [swissinfo.ch on the alphorn's history](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/culture/alphorn/7684720) (popular; instrument history only)
- **Sharing check: open.** A Swiss folk legend retold by the national public broadcaster,
  with no religious restriction. I did not find the original 19th-century collection it
  comes from; cite swissinfo.
- **Imagery:**
  - Safe: an alpine hut, a wooden horn, cowbells, a crag answering.
  - Avoid: Swiss national symbols and tourist branding.
- **Game ideas:** A three-way choice (strength, land or music) is a natural opening dilemma,
  and music is the right answer. "Have the mountains begun to sing?" is a lovely line for a
  restored peak.

#### 2.5 Birds who taught the chant — Ethiopian highlands (Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo tradition, Saint Yared)
- **Tied to:** birdsong, sacred chant (*zema*), the Semien Mountains.
- **Summary:** Ethiopian Orthodox tradition holds that the 6th-century Saint Yared was
  taught sacred chant by three birds sent from paradise. The three chant modes, *Ge'ez*,
  *Ezel* and *Araray*, are linked to them. Some tellings say even birdsong falls into these
  modes. Yared is said to have ended his life as a recluse in the Semien Mountains.
- **Sources:**
  - [Mahibere Kidusan (EOTC association), "Feast of Saint Yared"](https://eotcmk.org/e/feast-of-saint-yared/) (community, church-affiliated)
  - [Wikipedia, Yared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yared) (encyclopaedia)
  - [The Reporter Ethiopia, "The fountain of music"](https://www.thereporterethiopia.com/4673/) (Ethiopian press)
- **Sharing check: open as a story, sensitive as a figure.** The church tells it publicly
  and it is a national point of pride. But Yared is a venerated saint of a living church,
  and the chant is liturgy.
  - Use only the motif of mountain birds who teach a three-mode song, never the saint, the
    liturgy, or recordings of zema.
- **Imagery:**
  - Safe: three birds on a highland escarpment.
  - Avoid: Ethiopian icon painting, processional crosses, church drums (*kebero*) and the
    prayer stick (*mekwamia*), all of which are liturgical.
- **Game ideas:** Three mountain birds whose calls are three scales or modes. This could set
  the modes a VCO can be quantised to. Keep it unnamed and non-saintly.

#### 2.6 Kinnara, the bird-human musicians of the Himalaya — Hindu and Buddhist (India, Nepal, Tibet; carried to Southeast Asia)
- **Tied to:** song, flute, dance; mountain dwellers between earth and heaven.
- **Summary:** Kinnaras are part-human, part-bird celestial musicians, known for song,
  flute and devoted love. They are described as living in the Himalaya. They appear in the
  Jataka tales (e.g. the Canda-kinnara Jataka, where a kinnari's devotion to her wounded
  husband brings Indra to heal him), and in temple reliefs at Borobudur and Prambanan.
- **Sources:**
  - [Rubin Museum, Project Himalayan Art glossary: kinnara](https://projecthimalayanart.rubinmuseum.org/glossary/kinnara/) (museum)
  - [Wikipedia, Kinnara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinnara) (encyclopaedia)
- **Sharing check: open as literature, with living-religion care.** The Jatakas and the
  temple art are widely published. The kinnara is a mythic class, not a worshipped deity.
  - Sources tie them to the Himalaya more strongly in popular retellings than in primary
    texts.
  - Thai and Cambodian classical dance keep living kinnari roles.
  - Do not use Tibetan tantric deities, protectors or gandharva cult imagery.
- **Imagery:**
  - Safe: a bird-bodied, human-faced singer, as a generic idea.
  - Avoid: copying Borobudur or Prambanan reliefs, and Buddhist iconographic conventions
    (halos, mudras, lotus thrones).
- **Game ideas:** A pair of bird-human singers on a high ledge, restored as a duet. This
  overlaps with the muni bird and Yared's birds, so choose one bird figure per biome.

#### Mountain shortlist (open)
1. **Yamabiko, the mountain echo (Japan).** Open, landscape-bound (cliff, rock), and
   directly a sound mechanic (echo or delay).
2. **The alphorn-givers (Swiss Alps).** Open folk legend, a peak that "begins to sing", an
   echo, and a choice of music over power. Fits the game's theme exactly.
- Reserve: Echo the oread (Greek), which is open but overlaps with yamabiko and Pan.
- Promising but needs community consultation: sirinu (Andes).

#### Mountain rejected
- **Apus and Pachamama (Andes):** living, worshipped mountain and earth beings with active
  offerings; not specifically music figures. Do not depict them as dormant carvings.
- **Tengu (Japan):** a mountain yōkai, but not a music figure.
- **Tibetan mountain deities and gandharva cult imagery:** a living religion with protector
  deities; no community source permits outside depiction.
- **Saint Yared as a figure:** a venerated saint. Only the unnamed bird motif is usable.
- **Caucasus (Nart sagas, Ossetian *fændyr*):** the harp's origin story, made from bones, is
  grim, and I did not source it here.
- **Rocky Mountains Indigenous figures:** no community source found that shares a music
  figure; songs and ceremonies are commonly restricted. Left out rather than guessed.
- **Charango-playing sirens in colonial churches (Bolivia):** a Christian church sculpture
  context. Known only second-hand (Turino, via search snippet).
