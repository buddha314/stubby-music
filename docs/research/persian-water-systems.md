# Research: Persian water systems as a source of game ideas

**Source:** DamiLee, "The Greatest Empire was built on This Secret...", YouTube, published
2026-11-05, 23 min: <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fyYo1jxj5Io>. Research booklet:
<https://damileeresearch.framer.website/persian-gardens>.

Read from the video's captions and the booklet on 2026-11-06, not checked against primary
sources. Spellings come from captions. The booklet says some of its scenes are explanations, not
surveyed sites. Treat figures as the video's, and check them before anything in the game or its
writing relies on them. Times below are approximate.

The video's argument: a qanat is more than a water pipe. Its limits force an agreement between
people and land about who gets what and when, and that agreement is what deep wells removed.

## Ideas for the game

These are ours, not the video's, and none is decided. Each could become its own change.

### Using the land

1. **Water you find by reading the land.** Dawn vapor, plant life and soil colour mark hidden water,
   tying discovery to the sunrise opening and to finding things by ear. A test well confirms it.
2. **World generation with a water band.** Springs and hidden water gather where mountains meet the
   mudflat, from the seed, giving a reason to walk toward the mountains.
3. **Slope as a choice.** Too steep scours and muddies the water; too flat silts up. This could feed
   viscosity and clarity (`add-fluid-quality-and-viscosity`).
4. **Underground streams.** Dig from the outlet back toward the source: a surface stream shows where
   the music runs, and a tunnel hides it. Shafts could be where you hear it from above.
5. **Danger and lamps.** A dying flame warns of bad air. Consider whether this fits the mood.

### Water makes climate

6. **Walled gardens as the light-quality engine.** Walls, shade trees and water in one place raise
   the light-quality field faster than scattered plants, as the visible bloom pattern.
7. **Windcatchers.** The opening's windstorm stops being only a hazard: wind plus water cools a
   garden. A machine that works because of the storm.
8. **Night as a resource.** Thermal lag and ice pits tie production to the day and night cycle.
   "Moonlight rooms" fit lunarpunk nights closely.

### Sharing as music

9. **Dividing by time, on the rack.** The strongest fit. A mirab is a sequencer: gates decide which
   channel flows when, so a watering schedule is a rhythm you patch. It needs nothing beyond
   Eurorack conventions, and every machine stays an instrument.
10. **Dividing by place.** A stone splitter is a passive ratio, with no rack. It gives a simple tool
    before the sequencer.
11. **Watermills.** A drop turns a wheel, and the water carries on: clean power from a stream.
12. **Calendars.** As in Bali, planting and watering times across a whole basin could be one shared
    clock, with pests or dieback if they fall out of step.

### Limits and story

13. **A finite water table.** Pumps are easy and on demand. Overdraw and the ground sinks for good.
14. **The cracked mudflat as a past mistake.** The opening's cracks could be subsidence: the old world
    pumped itself dry, and this game rebuilds the agreement. Silence fits a world that stopped.
15. **The walled garden as the goal.** A garden as the long-term goal, with the word's history as
    background.

## Tensions to resolve

- **Infinite mines.** AGENTS.md makes burblers and trees infinite, with no finite reserve. Ideas 13
  and 14 need a separate finite source, or a change to that rule.
- **Mud or water.** Streams begin as raw mud from burblers. Hidden clean water would change what
  cleaning is for, and the order the player learns things.
- **Terrain.** The ground is flat and generated. Slope, flooding and mills all need height, which
  `add-digging` has not designed.
- **Seasons.** The calendar has day and night, not seasons. Winter ice and cisterns need seasons or
  a night-based version.
- **Staying original and respectful.** This is a real culture's living history, and some qanats are
  in use or have failed recently. Take the ideas, and do not present the game as depicting Iran
  without researching it properly and deciding that deliberately.
