## Context

`docs/design/design-guide.md` (Mythic regions) sets the direction: music deities found
drawn or carved into rocks and trees, rejuvenated with song, in regions like their mythic
origins. The first setting is the Mojave and the US Southwest, perhaps blended with
Australian Aboriginal myth, both desert lands; Aboriginal songlines map country through
song much as the game's streams are staff music.

## Decisions

### D1. Only openly shared stories (user, 2026-11-18)
The game draws only on stories their communities share openly, each with its source
recorded. Not used: anything restricted (to initiates, to men or to women, or to a
community), sacred objects and ceremonies, and any figure whose sharing is unclear until
it is checked. Petroglyphs and rock art are sacred sites to the descendants of their
makers; the game's carvings are its own designs, not copies of real panels.

### D2. Who shares counts more than where it is printed
A story in a book or online is not thereby openly shared: prefer sources from the
community itself (tribal and nation websites, cultural centres, Aboriginal land councils
and art centres, works by community members) and say which kind each source is. Popular
use does not settle it either: Kokopelli is everywhere, and still gets the same check.

### D3. One entry per figure
Each entry gives: name and people; what the figure is tied to (sound, instrument,
landscape, creature, season); a short summary in our words; the source and its kind; the
sharing check (open, restricted, unclear) and why; the imagery it suggests, with its own
check; and game notes, kept apart from what
the source says.

### D4. Figures are grouped by landscape, not culture (user, 2026-11-18)
This replaces the first D4 (four culture-based groups: desert, Mediterranean, temperate
forest, tropical). The creatures are a product of their environment, so the research
starts from the **biome** and looks for every culture whose homeland is that kind of
landscape, anywhere in the world. Eight game biomes, merged from the WWF's 14 terrestrial
biomes (Olson et al. 2001) and its freshwater types:

| Game biome | WWF biomes | Landscape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Desert** (the start) | 13 deserts and xeric shrublands; endorheic basins | sand, salt and mud flats, dry lakebeds that refill |
| **Chaparral** | 12 Mediterranean forests, woodlands and scrub | shrubs, rocky sand, scrub oak |
| **Grassland and savanna** | 7, 8 grasslands, savannas and shrublands | open grass, scattered trees |
| **Broadleaf forest** | 4 temperate broadleaf and mixed; 2 tropical dry broadleaf | oak, ash, deciduous woods |
| **Conifer rainforest** | 5 temperate coniferous; 6 boreal | tall, wet, mossy conifers |
| **Tropical forest** | 1 tropical moist broadleaf | jungle |
| **Wetland** | 9 flooded grasslands; floodplain freshwaters | marsh, bog, reed beds around lakes |
| **Mountain** | 10 montane grasslands; 11 tundra (alpine); montane freshwaters | bare rock, alpine meadow, mountain lakes |

Peoples in this world live together well and intermingle, so a biome's figures may come
from any of its cultures. The whole game needs **about a dozen characters**: the research
looks wide, and the summary picks about two strong cleared candidates per biome. How
biomes sit on the map is `add-mythic-regions`'s design (`docs/research/biome-map-generation.md`).

### D5. Unnamed figures, imagery from their heritage (user, 2026-11-18)
Figures appear unnamed, as spirits of a place, never as a named deity of a people. Their
look draws on the visual heritage of the cultures their biome draws on. The sharing
check covers imagery as well as stories: a motif, style or pattern is used only when it is
openly shared, and some are not (Aboriginal designs can belong to a clan or a Dreaming,
and a design for one figure is not free for another). A figure looks similar to its heritage, not identical
(user, 2026-11-18): the game takes elements and styles, never a reproduction of a
particular work, panel or sacred object. Each entry records the
imagery it suggests and that imagery's own sharing check.

### D6. Essences, not gods; chosen as we go (user, 2026-11-18)
The game does not portray specific gods. A figure is the **essence** of a kind of being,
"Kokopelli before some humans called him Kokopelli": the wandering flute player, the echo,
the singer on the lake. Often several traditions met the same essence (the echo is both
Japanese yamabiko and Greek Echo; an instrument made from a beloved creature is Mongol,
Kazakh and Finnish), and the game's figure is the essence, not any one of them. The
research entries remain the evidence: where an essence was found, and the sharing check on
the stories and imagery that inform it.

An essence does not clear what its sources restrict. Its look still passes the imagery
check, and it never reproduces a recognisable sacred image: the desert's wandering flute
player would not be the humpbacked flute player of Southwest rock art.

Which essences ship is **decided as we go**, region by region, not in one pass: the note's
essence table records each one's status (candidate, chosen, set aside, needs consultation).

## Risks / Trade-offs

- Desk research cannot speak for a community. Entries marked open are the best reading of
  public, community-published sources; before a figure ships, the user may want review by
  people from that community. That is recorded as a task, not assumed done.
- Choosing only open stories may leave a region thin. Then the region has fewer figures,
  or the user decides otherwise; the note does not fill gaps by invention.
