## Context

`docs/design/design-guide.md` (Mythic regions) sets the direction: music deities found
drawn or carved into rocks and trees, rejuvenated with song, in regions like their mythic
origins. The first setting is the Mojave and the US Southwest, perhaps blended with
Australian Aboriginal myth, both desert lands; Aboriginal songlines map country through
song much as the game's streams are staff music.

## Decisions

### D1. Only openly shared stories (user, 2026-11-18)
The game draws only on stories their communities share openly, each with its source
recorded. Not used: anything restricted (to initiates, to men or to women, or to a
community), sacred objects and ceremonies, and any figure whose sharing is unclear until
it is checked. Petroglyphs and rock art are sacred sites to the descendants of their
makers; the game's carvings are its own designs, not copies of real panels.

### D2. Who shares counts more than where it is printed
A story in a book or online is not thereby openly shared: prefer sources from the
community itself (tribal and nation websites, cultural centres, Aboriginal land councils
and art centres, works by community members) and say which kind each source is. Popular
use does not settle it either: Kokopelli is everywhere, and still gets the same check.

### D3. One entry per figure
Each entry gives: name and people; what the figure is tied to (sound, instrument,
landscape, creature, season); a short summary in our words; the source and its kind; the
sharing check (open, restricted, unclear) and why; the imagery it suggests, with its own
check; and game notes, kept apart from what
the source says.

### D4. Regions are biome groups (user, 2026-11-18)
Similar biomes are grouped, because the creatures are a product of their environment,
with small regional differences:

| Group | Draws on |
| --- | --- |
| Desert | the US Southwest and Mojave; Aboriginal Australia |
| Mediterranean | the Mediterranean lands |
| Temperate forest | Ireland and England; the Pacific Northwest |
| Tropical | Africa; perhaps Southeast Asia |

Peoples in this world live together well and intermingle, so a region's figures may come
from any tradition in its group. The whole game needs **about a dozen characters**, so
roughly three per group: the research looks wide, and the summary picks the strongest
cleared candidates per group. How groups sit on the procedural map is
`add-mythic-regions`'s design.

### D5. Unnamed figures, imagery from their heritage (user, 2026-11-18)
Figures appear unnamed, as spirits of a place, never as a named deity of a people. Their
look draws on the visual heritage of the cultures their group comes from. The sharing
check covers imagery as well as stories: a motif, style or pattern is used only when it is
openly shared, and some are not (Aboriginal designs can belong to a clan or a Dreaming,
and a design for one figure is not free for another). A figure looks similar to its heritage, not identical
(user, 2026-11-18): the game takes elements and styles, never a reproduction of a
particular work, panel or sacred object. Each entry records the
imagery it suggests and that imagery's own sharing check.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- Desk research cannot speak for a community. Entries marked open are the best reading of
  public, community-published sources; before a figure ships, the user may want review by
  people from that community. That is recorded as a task, not assumed done.
- Choosing only open stories may leave a region thin. Then the region has fewer figures,
  or the user decides otherwise; the note does not fill gaps by invention.
